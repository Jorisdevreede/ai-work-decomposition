# 31-1 Home Health and Personal Care Aides; and Nursing Assistants, Orderlies, and Psychiatric Aides

O*NET minor group 31-1: 1 occupation with a classed task (Personal Care Aides), 1 automate and amplify task (0 automate, 1 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

The rest of this group's work is hands-on care. The one task here is help with letters and information for a client.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Explaining a letter: Lees Simpel, a free Dutch app, takes a photo of a letter and answers in plain language who sent it, what it says and what to do [S1][S2] E2. It was built on OpenAI's GPT-4 (2023 report) [S3].
- Typing correspondence by voice: Microsoft Word Dictate supports Dutch (Netherlands) as a preview language, with spoken punctuation ("punt", "komma"); Microsoft warns preview languages may be less accurate [S4] E2.
- Reading post aloud: the Envision App reads printed text aloud in over 60 languages for blind and low-vision users [S5] E2.
- Plain-language medical letters: three Belgian hospital groups (AZ Maria Middelares, UZ Brussel, ZAS) give patients an AI plain-language version next to the original report; doctors do not review it [S6] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Lees Simpel had nearly 90,000 downloads by September 2024 [S7]. That is a usage count, not a measured outcome. No study of comprehension or error rate was found.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Eliminate** at the source: the founder of Lees Simpel says the problem belongs with the sender [S7], and Binnenlands Bestuur argues the same [S2]. Any organisation can fix its own letters before it sends them.
- **Configure**: dictation in Word or on the phone [S4]; DigiD Machtigen so the aide acts on government portals under their own login [S8].
- **Deterministic plus a bounded model loop**: explaining a letter or drafting a reply. Check: the aide reads the original with the client before anything is sent or paid; dates, amounts and deadlines come from the original, not the summary, because AI summaries can miss things and invent answers [S2].
- **Agent**: not needed. **Stays manual**: consent, the conversation with the client, and deciding what to send.
- Today the glue is the client's paper post, phone calls and the aide's memory (E1, model knowledge). A reminder of deadlines in the client's own calendar (**notification**) covers most of it (E1).

## 4. Gates

- DigiD: never use the client's DigiD login; use DigiD Machtigen, where the helper logs in with their own DigiD and the client's BSN [S8].
- AVG: client letters carry health data, BSN and money matters. A care organisation that rolls out an AI letter tool at scale falls under the AP's mandatory DPIA list (large-scale health data; financial data) [S9]. In 2023 Lees Simpel sent letters to OpenAI servers, kept there for a month, and advised users to cover personal details first [S3].
- EU AI Act art. 50: a chatbot must tell users they are dealing with an AI system, from 2 August 2026 [S10]. Help with a client's own letters is not an Annex III high-risk area [S11] (E1, model knowledge on the reading).
- Reported risk: AI letter summaries can miss points and make up answers [S2].
