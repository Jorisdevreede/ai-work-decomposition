# 29-2 Health Technologists and Technicians

O*NET minor group 29-2: 16 occupations (e.g. lab and imaging technologists, pharmacy technicians, medical records specialists), 51 automate and amplify tasks (38 automate, 13 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source. Billing and DRG tasks are US-shaped (Dutch analogue: DBC and LBZ).

## 1. Products that do this today

- Images: Siemens AI-Rad Companion measures nodules and brain volumes [S1]; DeepHealth Lung (Aidence, Amsterdam) tracks lung nodules [S2]; Siemens teamplay Protocols distributes scanner protocols [S3] E2.
- Karyograms: ASI HiBand classifies chromosomes [S4] E2.
- EEG: SCORE-AI classifies routine EEGs [S5] E2.
- Prescriptions: Dutch pharmacy systems run G-Standaard surveillance (interactions, contra-indications, dose) with KNMP minimum settings [S6] E2.
- Dispensing: OLVG's APOTECAchemo robot compounds cytostatics [S7]; Diakonessenhuis robots fill medication rolls [S8] E2.
- Coding: DHD codes day admissions to ICD-10 from clinical text in 32 hospitals [S9] E2.
- Transcription: Autoscriber summarises consultations into ChipSoft HiX [S10]; Jeroen Bosch Ziekenhuis too [S11] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- DHD: 65% of day admissions coded with high certainty; codes above 70% skip the coder, 85 to 90% correct; Rijnstate 72% automatic [S9] E3, provider figures.
- Three Canadian labs: middleware rules lifted autoverification from 40-60% to over 90%; each held sample then took 21 s, not 7 [S12] E3.
- OLVG: twice the output per employee, direct labour cost 2.5 times lower; the pharmacist's own figure [S7] E3.
- Dutch GPs, ambient scribe: 42.7 s less documentation per consultation, consultation length unchanged [S13] E3.
- AON lab (US), HiBand: analysis time down 35 to 40% [S4] E3.
- SCORE-AI: 88.3% accuracy on 60 benchmark EEGs, similar to experts [S5] E3.
- GPT-4 MRI protocols: from fixed lists, under 1% unacceptable; freely composed, 76.5% accepted in-house, 46.7% elsewhere [S14] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Data entry, logs, archiving, retrieval, 17 of 51 tasks: integrations (analyser to LIS, PACS, e-prescription) plus a records app replace paper logs, spreadsheets and fax (banned for sharing) [S15].
- Result and prescription checks, 3 tasks: rules [S6][S12]. Charges, claims, refraction, menu nutrients from NEVO [S16]: deterministic (E1).
- Coding, 3 tasks: bounded model loop with a confidence threshold; coders review the rest [S9].
- Reports, 7 tasks: structured templates; ISCN strings from structured karyotype data (E1); a bounded model loop drafts summaries; check: clinician reads before filing [S11].
- Requests to protocols, 2 tasks: model picks from a fixed list; technologist confirms [S14].
- Patient representatives, 8 tasks: answer desk on policy texts, complaint workflow with deadline notifications [S17]; conversations stay human (E1).
- Agent: not needed.

## 4. Gates

- MDR: medical-purpose software is a device [S18]; Sweden's regulator put Tandem's AI scribe at class IIa or higher, not I (preliminary; vendor page) [S19].
- AI Act: MDR and IVDR are Annex I [S20]; AI there is high-risk if a notified body must assess it [S21], from 2 August 2028 [S22][S23]. Patient chatbots disclose AI since 2 August 2026 [S22].
- AVG: health data are special category (art. 9) [S24]; large-scale processing needs a DPIA [S25].
- WGBO: keep the dossier 20 years; patients may ask who viewed it [S15].
- Wet BIG: ionising radiation is a reserved act; others act on assignment with instructions [S26].
- Wkkgz: answer complaints within six weeks, plus four [S17].
- WOR 27(1)(l): recording consultations or logging coder corrections can count as staff monitoring [S27].
- Risks: inaccurate summaries, patients holding back sensitive information [S13]; protocol accuracy drops at another hospital [S14]; IGJ guidelines assume manual compounding [S7].
