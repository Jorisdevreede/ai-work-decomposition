# Digest 53-5: Water Transportation Workers

DIGEST-A, 2026-10-15. 2 automate/amplify O*NET tasks across 2 occupations (sailors and marine oilers, ship engineers): writing the ship's log, and the engineering log or bell book.
Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **NAPA Logbook (NAPA, Finland), electronic logbooks:** deck, engine, GMDSS, ballast water and MARPOL logbooks, with entries filled from automation and sensor data; accepted by more than 20 flag states, the Netherlands among them; some flags limit which logbooks may be electronic [S1]. Covers both tasks. E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- **Anthony Veder, NAPA Logbook on 20 vessels from 2023:** against a 2020 baseline of 13,875 paperwork hours per vessel per year, the first wave of engine-room logbook automation saved 8,000 hours across the fleet, an average 14% less time on paperwork. The period is not stated, and the headline "2,000 hours per vessel" does not match these figures [S2]. E3, vendor case, Feb 2025.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Ship's log: weather, distance (1 task):** integration, deterministic. Position, distance run and wind come from navigation and weather sensors; the officer of the watch checks and signs (E1).
- **Engine log and bell book (1 task):** integration with the alarm and monitoring system and the engine telegraph, so readings (rpm, voltage) and speed or direction orders are written with a timestamp; the engineer adds remarks and signs (E1).
- No model step is needed beyond free-text remarks; agent needed: none.

## 4. Gates

- **IMO MEPC.312(74), 2019:** electronic record books are an alternative to paper for MARPOL record books (oil, cargo, garbage, ozone, fuel changeover, engine parameters), approved by the flag state; the guidelines set terms for audit logging and backup [S3]. Deck and engine logs follow each flag's own rules.
- **Inland navigation, Directive (EU) 2017/2397 art. 22:** journeys go in the logbook, one active logbook per craft; electronic logbooks are allowed where put in place; the Commission's assessment of tamper-proof electronic logbooks was due 17 January 2026 [S4].
- **WOR art. 27 (k, l):** logs that record who did what and when could be used to check crew performance; that use needs works council consent [S5].
- **EU AI Act:** no Annex III area applies to log keeping [S6].
- **Reported failures and risks:** flag acceptance is uneven; some flags limit which logbooks may be electronic [S1].
