# Digest 33-9: Other Protective Service Workers

DIGEST-A, 2026-10-15. 11 automate/amplify O*NET tasks across 3 occupations (private detectives 7, retail loss prevention specialists 3, animal control workers 1).
Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **FRISS, claims fraud scoring:** scores each insurance claim with reasons and routes doubtful ones to a person; a module structures fact-building on flagged claims [S1]. E2.
- **Stichting CIS (Netherlands), shared insurer databank:** almost 400 insurers and agents use it in acceptance and claims decisions [S2]. E2.
- **Maltego, OSINT and link analysis:** searches social media, breach and identity data and draws link graphs, for locating persons [S3]. E2.
- **Veesion (France), shop camera analysis:** flags suspicious gestures in camera feeds; staff decide whether it is theft. Vendor claims "up to" 60% lower theft losses, 5,000+ shops [S4]. E2.
- **Amivedi (Netherlands), lost and found pet register:** volunteer register linked with police, vets, animal ambulances and shelters; 231 reports a day [S5]. E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- **UNIQA (insurer), FRISS:** USD 21 million fraud savings in the first two years live; savings per investigator rose from USD 550,000 to USD 2 million [S1]. E3, vendor case.
- None found for report drafting, background checks or retail incident tools; the shared search budget ran out early.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Reports and case files (4 tasks, impoundment files included):** records app first. The privacy code already fixes the dossier fields: assignment, method and reason, findings, photos [S6]. Fill them as work happens; a template assembles the report. A bounded model step may draft the narrative from logged facts. Check: each sentence points to a log entry. Stop: the investigator signs (E1).
- **Searches and background checks (3 tasks):** register integrations (Chamber of Commerce, Land Registry, insolvency register) behind a proportionality field filled before any search (E1). A model may summarise found documents; it does not pick targets.
- **Fraud and embezzlement (2 tasks):** existing scoring tools; ledger queries for duplicate payments and odd amounts are plain code (E1); judging intent stays human.
- **Loss-reduction advice and paid investigations (2 tasks):** a dashboard over incidents and stock losses feeds the advice; both stay human.
- **Agent:** none. An autonomous OSINT agent clashes with the least-intrusive-method rule [S6].
- **Glue:** Word reports, Excel and Access incident logs, mail to clients. Replace with one case register and a report export (E1).

## 4. Gates

- **Wpbr:** a recherchebureau needs a Justis licence; investigators are screened and need a diploma [S7]. Rpbr art. 23a makes the sector privacy code binding [S6].
- **Privacy code:** least intrusive method, proportionality weighed first; register data kept one to five years; the person investigated is told, at the latest once the client has the report; health data only where needed next to criminal data [S6].
- **AI Act Art. 5 (since 2 February 2025):** bans building face databases by untargeted scraping, emotion recognition at work, biometric categorisation by race or politics, and crime prediction based only on profiling [S8]. Face matching in shops falls under Annex III area 1; AI pre-employment checks under 4(a) [S9].
- **GDPR Art. 9:** biometric data used to identify a person is special category [S10].
- **WOR art. 27(l):** shop cameras and loss analytics also watch staff; works council consent first [S11].
- **Reported failures and risks:** the code warns hidden cameras quickly intrude and cites Criminal Code articles 139f and 441b [S6]. No reported failure opened in this run.
