# 29-9 Medical registrars, genetic counselors, midwives and athletic trainers

DIGEST-C, 2026-10-15. 29 automate/amplify tasks across 4 occupations (health information technologists and medical registrars, genetic counselors, midwives, athletic trainers); trainers' tasks here are records and claims. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **AI-ondersteund coderen (Dutch Hospital Data, NL)**: a model reads specialists' EPD notes and proposes the ICD-10 main diagnosis for day admissions, with a confidence score [S1]. Task: "identify, compile, abstract, and code patient data, using standard classification systems". E2
- **Autoscriber (NL)**: drafts structured consultation notes into HiX, Nedap ONS and other EHRs; NEN 7510 certified; users include LUMC and Rijnstate [S2]. Task: "write detailed consultation reports". E2
- **mijnPerined (Perined, NL)**: midwives register in their own software, deliver the national dataset and compare their own figures with others [S3]. Task: "compile and evaluate clinical practice statistics". E2

## 2. Cases with numbers

- **Dutch Hospital Data, 2024 to 2026**: 32 hospitals use the coding model. On average it codes about 65% of day admissions above a 70% confidence threshold that CBS approved for acceptance without a coder (Rijnstate: 72%). Accuracy is 85 to 90%, which CBS judged comparable to a medical coder [S1]. It coded over 165,000 day admissions in 2024, trained federated so data stays in each hospital [S4]. Measured by DHD, the provider; no independent audit found. E3

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Statistics and registry reports** ("census data ... use of hospital beds", "tumor registry data", "clinical practice statistics"): dashboards on data that already moves through fixed pipelines: LBZ data leaves the EPD through an LBZ module and ZorgTTP [S5]; midwives get figures back in mijnPerined [S3]. Deterministic.
- **Coding and DRG assignment** ("assign the patient to diagnosis-related groups"): configure first: clinicians pick thesaurus terms already linked to ICD-10 [S6]; the DBC grouper is rule software (E1). Free text gets the DHD loop: model proposes, auto-accept above the threshold, below it a coder's worklist, audit samples [S1].
- **Tumor registry abstraction**: IKNL data managers still collect items from the record [S7]; a bounded step could pre-fill items with the source sentence for them to confirm (E1).
- **Consultation letters and contact notes**: ambient draft, clinician signs. Check: variant names and risk figures must match the lab report; a mismatch blocks sending (E1).
- **Patient information** ("explain diagnostic procedures"): an answer desk quoting approved leaflets. Configure.
- **Legislation watch** ("monitor changes in legislation"): a feed of changed articles with a linked summary (E1).
- **Glue today** (E1): coding worklists and registry extracts in Excel, letters dictated to secretaries.
- **Stays with a person**: family risk analysis, counselling, birth care.

## 4. Gates

- **WGBO**: the dossier is kept 20 years; a signed AI draft is part of it [S8].
- **GDPR art. 9**: genetic and health data are special category [S9]. LBZ deliveries pass ZorgTTP's privacy module and feed CBS and NZa [S5].
- **Wet BIG**: midwives are BIG-registered [S10]; they own their records.
- **MDR**: software giving information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is class IIa or higher (rule 11) [S11]. Attendi is certifying its summaries as class IIa because care plans build on them [S12]; genetic risk tools likely fall here (E1). AI inside such devices is high-risk under the AI Act from 2 August 2028 [S13].
- **EU AI Act annex III**: no point applies; coding, registries and patient information are not eligibility decisions by public authorities (5(a)) [S14]. Patient-facing chat must say it is AI (art. 50) [S15].
