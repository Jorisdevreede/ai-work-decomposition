# 27-3 Media and Communication Workers

O*NET minor group 27-3: 9 occupations (e.g. reporters, editors, PR specialists, technical writers, translators), 92 automate and amplify tasks (62 automate, 30 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source. The court reporter task is US-specific (E1).

## 1. Products that do this today

- Translation terminology: DeepL glossaries enforce preferred terms across 100+ language pairs [S1] E2.
- Proofreading Dutch: LanguageTool checks spelling, grammar, punctuation and style, and rephrases [S2] E2.
- House style: Acrolinx (now Markup AI) scores text against a style guide, repeatably, with quality gates [S3] E2.
- Transcripts, subtitles: Amberscript sells Dutch machine ("90%+ accurate") and human-checked ("99%+") output [S4] E2.
- PR monitoring: Coosto (Eindhoven) tracks conversations and news topics and schedules posts [S5] E2.
- Claim checking: Full Fact AI flags checkable claims and matches repeats; used by 40+ fact-checkers in 30 countries [S6] E2.
- Readability, reader questions: Tubantia suggests simpler wording; De Volkskrant tests an archive chatbot [S7] E2.
- First-line news: Mediahuis (about 25 titles) trials agents that pick, write, legally check and fact-check stories; a person publishes [S8] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- MIT trial, 444 professionals writing press releases, reports and emails: ChatGPT cut time 37%, grades rose 0.45 SD. Working paper [S9] E3.
- Roche (Acrolinx): 30% fewer translation edits; a device maker: reviews in two days, not three weeks. Vendor case [S3] E3.
- DeepL: translations 90% faster, workload halved. Forrester composite commissioned by DeepL [S10] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Editing and proofreading (about 27 of 92 lines):** spell checkers, style rules and term lists first [S2][S3]; model rewrites arrive as tracked changes.
- **Drafting (about 30 lines):** bounded model loop: model drafts, each fact traces to a source, style checker scores, a second person approves. Creative writing (12 lines) stays manual (E1).
- **Research and fact checks (about 9):** answer desk over the archive [S7]; claim matching [S6].
- **Translation (8):** memory and term base are deterministic, machine translation the bounded step, a translator post-edits (E1).
- **Wire selection and monitoring (about 5):** dashboard filters, then a judgement step. ANP tests turning articles into radio scripts with Utrecht University [S11].
- **Logs and admin (about 8):** playout logs, revision history, scheduled posts, indexes: configuration (E1).
- Mail and attachments carry press releases and copy today; a CMS workflow replaces them (E1). Agents: only Mediahuis-style pipelines [S8].

## 4. Gates

- **AI Act art. 50(4):** public-interest text made by AI needs a label unless a person reviews it under editorial responsibility; synthetic audio and video need disclosure [S12]. Applies since 2 August 2026; existing generators must mark output from 2 December 2026 [S13][S14]. AI meant to sway voting is high-risk (Annex III 8(b)) [S15] from 2 December 2027 [S13][S14].
- **NVJ model guideline (2026):** final check by another person; no confidential or personal data in AI tools; AI use reported to the editorial council; no output tracking [S16], which would also need works council consent (WOR 27(1)(l)) [S17].
- **AVG:** UAVG art. 43 lifts most duties for purely journalistic processing [S18]; PR and translation get none (E1).
- **Copyright:** text and data mining is allowed unless the maker opted out machine-readably (Auteurswet 15o) [S19]. Over 30 Dutch news brands opened archives to GPT-NL [S7].
- **Failures:** ex-NRC editor Peter Vandermeersch was suspended for AI-invented quotes in 15 of 53 blog posts (March 2026) [S20]. In an EBU/BBC study with NOS, 45% of 3,000+ AI-assistant news answers had a significant issue [S21].
