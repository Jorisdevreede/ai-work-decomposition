# Digest 43-5: Material Recording, Scheduling, Dispatching, and Distributing Workers

DIGEST-A, 2026-10-15. 47 automate/amplify O*NET tasks across 9 occupations (freight forwarders and agents, planning, shipping and dispatch clerks, others).
Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **Qargo TMS with TransFollow eCMR (TransFollow, Netherlands), documents:** eCMRs created and signed inside the TMS [S1]; AI reads PDF orders into fields [S2]. Covers bills of lading, records, invoices. E2.
- **Portbase (Rotterdam), port messages:** prenotifies customs documents to terminals, shows import status, files T1 transit; vendor says 15,500 users, 94 million messages a year [S3]. E2.
- **Eurora, used by DPD, tariff codes:** automates HS code allocation and export-control checks [S4]. E2.
- **Boltrics 3PL Dynamics (Netherlands), records:** WMS, TMS, forwarding and customs software [S5]. E2.
- **EcoTransIT World, emissions:** ISO 14083 emissions per transport chain, five modes, with an API [S6]. E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- **Mark Huisman Logistics (NL), Qargo:** invoicing from 10 hours a week to 30 minutes since go-live, 1 January 2026 [S7]. E3, vendor case.
- **FCE Transport (NL), Qargo:** order entry from 3-4 minutes to about 1 per job; invoices out in 1-2 days, not a week; no period [S2]. E3, vendor case.
- **Cross Freight (Hamburg, 35 staff), Stargo:** 80% less manual data entry per shipment, 60% faster customs handling; no baseline or period [S8]. E3, vendor case.
- **Smurfit Kappa Hoya plant (DE), Transporeon time slots:** truck turnaround from 120 to 52 minutes; undated [S9]. E3, vendor case.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Documents, records, work orders (12 tasks):** records app plus artefact generator, configure existing software. One order record prints every document and the eCMR. A bounded model reads emailed orders into fields, checked against the customer master; low confidence goes to a clerk.
- **Rates, weights, charges (5 tasks):** rate cards in the TMS, plain code (E1).
- **Customs (5 tasks):** DMS integration through customs software; codes from the item master. For new items a model proposes three codes; a declarant picks (E1).
- **Routes, modes, consolidation, emissions (10 tasks):** solver plus emissions API; the planner judges special cargo. Carrier negotiation stays a conversation.
- **Status and customer questions (5 tasks):** notifications from track events; an answer desk quoting the firm's rules, else handover.
- **Planning and dispatch schedules (7 tasks):** configure ERP planning (SAP); solver re-runs on shortages; clerk approves.
- **Call records, shipment checks (3 tasks):** system logs; label-manifest-order matching is plain code.
- **Glue:** Excel and Outlook carry orders between customers, TMS, carriers and customs; API links, eCMR and Portbase replace them. Agent needed: none.

## 4. Gates

- **Union Customs Code:** DMS replaces AGS to meet UCC deadlines (import 31 December 2022, export 1 December 2023); phase two slipped for lack of IT capacity [S10].
- **eFTI and eCMR:** authorities must accept certified eFTI platform data from 9 July 2027 [S11]. The Benelux counted 400,000 digital consignment notes in 2020 [S12]; abroad, drivers may need paper [S13].
- **EU AI Act:** documents, customs and routing touch no Annex III area. Scheduling staff by behaviour or traits is 4(b); call records become 5(d) only if a tool classifies calls or sets priority [S14]; from 2 December 2027 [S15]. Answer desks must say they are AI [S16].
- **WOR art. 27:** scheduling rules on working times need consent (b); tools logging who did what, (k, l) [S17].
- **Reported failures and risks:** in a vendor's customs case, brokers accepted the top HS suggestion unchanged 87% of the time [S18].
