**Review of the team-reimagine method before tonight's fleet**

Changes, most important first.

1. **Split the build ladder from the operating mode.** D, D+ and A say who runs the workflow (code, code with bounded model calls, a model). Copilot says who leads (the person). They are different axes, and a briefing copilot is usually built as D+. Make `build` an enum {eliminate, configure existing software, D, D+, A} and keep `autonomy_level` plus a new `authority` (read, write, post, pay) as the second axis. Add eliminate (Hammer: re-keying an invoice that will arrive as UBL is not an llm_step) and configure (the bookkeeper sample says "AFAS already does it with rules"; that is cheaper than D and the Tenex rule says buy first). Reason: today's vote-then-override is a count hack; with a D-first prompt every model will say D anyway and the override becomes moot.

2. **Ask every model to fill the whole ladder per task, not pick one rung.** For each task: the D version and what it cannot handle; the D+ version with each loop's input, output, check and stop condition; whether A is needed and why. Then the choice is a structured comparison (Torres' three solutions) and "the last 20%" falls out naturally as "what D could not handle and what the loop does with it". Reason: it kills the pattern vote problem in [findings.md](file:///Users/Joris.deVreede/Projects/agentlabs-hub/solutions/team-reimagine/method/findings.md) (26 agent-consensus tasks with a simpler vote) and forces the model to argue, which is where sameness breaks.

3. **Derive time saved from steps, not from a single percentage.** All three models land on 50%. Ask per step of `today.steps`: share of time, and fate {gone, machine, person reviews, person does}, with a review-time estimate for "person reviews". Compute time_saved in the script. Reason: one anchored number is the weakest field you have and the whole roll-up hangs on it; the step split is checkable by the person at intake and gives you the review time Mollick says to subtract.

4. **Make the night output structured facts, and generate prose at intake.** The prompt in [reimagine.py](file:///Users/Joris.deVreede/Projects/agentlabs-hub/solutions/team-reimagine/tools/reimagine.py) is company-specific (systems, other roles, CAO). On 4,400 O*NET tasks there is no company, so seven lens lines per task will be generic and nobody reads 35,000 lines. Keep per task: the ladder (change 2), step fates (3), human_edge, legal gate flags, evidence, build effort, run cost class, and two or three short lens facts as enums or ten-word lines. Then one cheap call per person at intake writes the narrative with their systems, drudgery tags and HAS slider. Reason: that is where the sameness and the Dutch specificity both get solved, and the de-slop pass then runs on far less text.

5. **Fix the jev probes for a deterministic-first bet.** "Rules" bunches on "partly" (190 of 268), so `codify ≥ 0.5` almost never fires and the rule undersupplies D. Replace it with "share of cases written rules would handle" (under half, half to 80, 80 to 95, over 95) and "what makes the rest hard" (missing data, judgement, people, odd documents). Add: interface (API or export versus screen only; METR's 40 to 100× shorter horizon), "does standard software already do this", the frontier probe (would a wrong answer look confident), and the obliterate probe (would this task exist if the input arrived structured). Use `accountable` to cap autonomy at 3; it is collected and never read in [pattern_rules.py](file:///Users/Joris.deVreede/Projects/agentlabs-hub/solutions/team-reimagine/tools/pattern_rules.py). Drop model anatomy from the schema; jev's core answer covers it.

6. **Replace self-reported confidence with measured agreement.** Priority uses `confidence`, which the model grades itself (Sol low on everything, Opus high). Use: model-versus-model agreement, jev-versus-model agreement, evidence grade, and self-consistency (change 7). Reason: priority is what the customer acts on.

7. **Pilot before the fleet, and measure self-consistency.** Run 20 batches, read 40 tasks, fix the prompt, then run the rest. Run Opus twice on 100 tasks with shuffled batches. If Opus-versus-Opus agreement is near Opus-versus-Grok, the second model buys nothing and Grok's templated, unlinked E2s (327) are not worth the merge cost. Batches of 8 from one occupation homogenise; use 4 or shuffle across occupations within a minor group.

8. **Digest checks.** Minor-group digests are generic; the E4 effect came from role digests with Dutch products. Give every digest the same four blocks (products with opened links, cases with numbers, the deterministic-first advice, legal gates). Verify every E2/E3 source string appears in the digest, and run the link checker over all of it. Reason: 82% citing the digest is also an anchoring risk; a citation that is not in the digest is invented.

9. **Add build effort and run cost to the priority denominator, and point versus system.** Value × confidence ÷ time to value with copilot × 0.5 still rewards cheap builds; now that days and cost class exist, use them. Also name the constraint step per value stream (Goldratt); hours freed at a non-constraint are not throughput.

10. **Provenance per field.** Model, prompt hash, digest version, de-slop touched or not. Show jev and model disagreement as a flag on the card. That, plus a human-reviewed sample of 100 tasks with inter-rater scores, is the minimum before a customer sees it.

11. **Beating the spreadsheet.** Three things a spreadsheet cannot do: per person, refreshed monthly; a runnable golden demo per opportunity; and a measured loop, where the first build reports its touchless share and review time back into the model (the first E4). Plan the measurement hooks into the build brief now.

**Risks in the bets**

- **Undiscounted potential with a 50/80/100 ramp** assumes every build gets built and adopted. Label it as the bet it is, show the field-study band next to it, and let the customer's first measurement replace both.
- **Deterministic-first can over-claim.** The current failure is agents everywhere; the new one will be D on messy input. Keep a detector: D claimed while jev says unstructured input or rules under half, send to review.
- **"10 to 100× faster to build"** moves the cost to data access, works-council consent and integration. Build effort in days should include those, or the backlog will promise two-week deliveries that take a quarter.
- **De-slop is cosmetic.** The sameness is at idea level ("every case", "owns the rules"). Flag those phrases as ideas, not words, and rewrite with "name one specific thing".
