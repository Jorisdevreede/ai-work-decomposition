# Recruiter review synthesis

**1. Combined ranking**

The majority order, shared by the Fable and Opus reviewers, is **Fable > Opus > Grok > Sol > Astra**. There is no unanimous ranking.

Shared observations explain the trade-offs: Fable makes the working week concrete, but its opportunity hours need repair. Opus offers useful builder detail, but inconsistent review gates. Grok contributes concrete deterministic checks, but overstates savings. Sol and Astra have sound arithmetic and explicit upkeep, but less accessible prose and demos.

Astra’s reviewer instead prefers Astra overall, then Sol for implementation; its recruiter ranking puts Opus first. The disagreement is mainly whether vivid, usable proposals outweigh stronger action controls and accounting boundaries.

**2. Ten ideas to keep**

1. **Fable:** Give each flex order a reference that Finance uses to match invoices.
2. **Fable:** Require vacancy must-haves before opening recruitment.
3. **Opus/Fable/Grok:** Show quoted evidence and missing criteria, never a suitability score; route uncertainty to a person.
4. **Astra/Sol:** Bind sends to approved versions, cancel stale messages and ignore duplicate events.
5. **Astra:** Reconcile conflicting agency CSV/email confirmations before committing an order.
6. **Grok:** Block agency orders with missing pay fields; check certificate dates against training records.
7. **Fable/Grok:** Answer from approved vacancy facts; flag unsupported replies and check every figure.
8. **Fable:** Turn a missing certificate into a training route where the site offers training.
9. **Sol:** Test extraction against instructions embedded maliciously in a CV.
10. **Grok/Opus/Astra/Sol:** Pair hiring speed with retention; Astra/Sol also preserve productivity, denominators and missing outcomes.

**3. Errors, grouped**

- **Accounting:** Basic sums pass. Fable’s opportunity totals overlap or cannot be reproduced. Astra also finds overlapping test administration in Fable/Opus. Grok credits savings against steps already labelled as performed by a system today. Savings remain assumptions, not measured results.
- **Design:** Fate, rung and gate fields conflict in Fable/Opus/Grok. Astra allows zero exception time for scheduling. Opus/Grok include weakly justified model calls. No answer uses autonomous agents.
- **Law:** Reviewers flag Grok’s automatic replies and deterministic checks labelled high-risk; Opus flags missed targeted-ad duties across all five. Astra challenges blanket exemptions and Article 22 claims. Works-council grounds remain disputed. Fable’s reviewer trusts the 2027 deferral; Opus/Astra say verification is incomplete.
- **Evidence and inputs:** Source IDs conflict between digest and signals. Sol’s downgrading is criticised by Fable but defended by Opus/Astra. Unsupported company facts, inaccurate Jev summaries and Fable/Opus word-limit breaches also appear. Product/source invention was not established.

**4. Where Jev helped or failed**

Useful signals include low model need for routine messages, sensitive data, reversibility, rules and error costs. Whole-task “human” labels hide automatable paperwork; occupation-based API guesses contradict company email/CSV access. Interview “vanishes” is misleading. Matching remains disputed: Fable reports agreement on extraction, while Opus distinguishes checklist-first designs from gated judgement. Astra warns that category probabilities establish neither savings nor legal permission, and model agreement is not validation.

**5. Method changes**

Counts are distinct reviewers requesting each change, out of three, including requests outside their method section.

- **Schema:** Shared workflow identities/ownership, new/transferred work, named approvers and validation/example repairs **[3]**; separate current/future actors, scoped gate objects and rule-set legal tags **[2 each]**.
- **Jev questions:** Use actual company access and permissions **[3]**; derive class from steps, reword “vanishes”, ask human-core share and local lawfulness **[2 each]**.
- **Prompt:** One source-ID scheme, concrete demos and dated legal guidance **[3 each]**; explicit opportunity ownership/mappings **[2]**; test withholding Jev to measure anchoring **[1]**.
- **Merge:** Align workflows **[3]**. Majority fates/median estimates **[1: Fable]**, strictest gates **[1: Opus]**, coherent designs rather than voting **[1: Astra]** conflict.
- **Roll-up:** Include upkeep and separate potential from adoption **[3]**; scripted arithmetic and step-based ceilings **[2]**; distinguish capacity, cash and throughput **[1]**.

**6. Full-run advice**

Fable’s reviewer recommends Opus generation, Fable role synthesis and sampled Sol/Astra review. Opus recommends Fable generation, Opus if cheaper, Sol review and a second generator on 5%. Both reserve Grok for disputes or shortlists. Astra recommends provisional Sol generation with targeted Astra review after broader calibration. All favour mechanical validation and selective second-model spending over repeated full generation. No cost/quality winner is established by this pilot.
