The hard part was deciding where one task ends. Reviewing an application, selecting an applicant and interviewing all consume the same dossier. Scheduling and developing tests overlap too. I assigned application extraction to 18868, candidate assessment sessions to 18869, assessment design to 1002, and hiring decisions and offer handoff to 18871. Shared artefacts connect them. This is an accounting convention to validate with practitioners, not a discovered Noordhaven process.

The input shares sum to one, but rounded hours_per_fte sum to 1,600.1. I used shares against the supplied 3,200 hours. That prevents a small reconciliation error from entering every opportunity. Step shares still have much greater uncertainty: no observation establishes how long recruiters spend reading, discussing or correcting. More detailed arithmetic makes assumptions inspectable; it does not make them measured.

I did not translate jev probabilities into savings. Its human labels conceal administration around assessments and references. Its API probability for agencies conflicts with the actual email/CSV input. Its low rule coverage for selection describes judgement, while document assembly is straightforward. Conversely, a “deterministic” label can hide the human effort needed to keep vacancy facts current. The operating reserve exposes some of that burden, but its size is also an E0 assumption.

The schema forces today's steps to carry tomorrow's tools, authority and approval. A draft may need review before another action sends it, but one Boolean cannot describe approval identity, scope, expiry or invalidation. There is no workflow edge, shared-cost owner, new-work row or structured outcome metric. I put these details in assumptions and prose. This works for twelve tasks and becomes fragile at 4,400. I followed the strict schema rather than copying the example's extra metadata and derived fields; some example steps also label work “machine” while retaining human time.

The evidence supports capabilities and cautions, not these hours. I reopened the cited product, process and legal pages. The AI Act explorer and Praxikon corroborated the deferral, but the cited Official Journal page supplied no usable text. The AP and Dutch statute pages also failed to render. Their unavailable text is not independent verification. Product marketing establishes neither recruiter acceptance nor fair selection. Digest and signals source numbers differ, making silent citation errors easy.

Before scaling, I would make three changes:

1. **Represent workflows and quantities explicitly.** Give each shared step a stable ID, baseline owner and source. Separate current and proposed steps. Add volumes, measured minutes, new work, transferred work, costs and approval objects. Permit uncertainty and increased effort. Compute role totals and opportunity totals from the same owned steps.

2. **Calibrate before exposing scores.** Have practitioners label a stratified sample, including human tasks and awkward interfaces. Estimate steps once without jev and once with it. Measure review and exception time in pilots. Distinguish low confidence from missing data, and show cohort maturity, manager queues and peak coverage beside labour capacity.

3. **Make evidence and release checks mechanical.** Use stable source IDs, retrieved excerpts, dates and claim-level limits. Independently check consequential legal claims. Validate schema, cross-field rules, arithmetic and duplicate ownership before publication. Require each opportunity's demo to test a failure and feed measured results back into its assumptions. Use additional model review for consequential disagreements, not as proof that an estimate is true.
