# Reflection

The difficult part was separating work that disappears from work that somebody else must still do. A manager decision, a specialist test validation and an agency negotiation remain necessary even when a recruiter stops copying fields. Task labels obscure that distinction. Booking assessments offers substantial coordination savings inside a human task; candidate matching still needs human judgement inside an automate task.

The twelve tasks are not twelve independent workflows. Application review creates evidence used by interviews and selection. Criteria review governs tests but does not validate each item. I assigned extraction to application review, interview recording to interviews, test-kit upkeep to test development and per-candidate administration to assessment scheduling. Candidate sends do not claim decision savings. Agency coordination excludes invoice reconciliation. Those boundaries matter more than another decimal place in the speedup.

The step shares are the weakest numbers. The input gives role shares but not observed processes, case volumes, event reliability or time logs. I proposed plausible baselines and remaining touch time, including exceptions and approvals, then derived savings. There is no E4 evidence. The 80-hour upkeep allowance and adoption ramp are also assumptions. The first pilot should time review, exception handling, rework and monitoring before expanding. More applications can consume returned capacity even when effort per case falls.

Sources introduced another uncertainty. The digest and signals use different source numbering. I used digest IDs consistently. Network access could not reopen product or legal pages. The task graph therefore grades their supplied claims E1 here, and the job does not rely on the digest’s claimed legal deferral. Independent checks of current primary law, AFAS release claims and Recruitee features remain necessary. A vendor case about shorter hiring stages does not establish Noordhaven labour savings.

The schema helps by forcing steps, authority and bounded-call checks. It also blurs machine output with person labour: a step can send externally while the current actor remains recruiter. A boolean approval cannot say which approver, what was reviewed, when approval expires or whether a standing policy covers the action. The time model cannot represent added monitoring as its own task, negative savings or work transferred to another role. Its three-to-seven-step limit makes exception branches disappear into prose. Its evidence enum excludes E4, and it has no place for source excerpts, retrieval status or claim-specific legal scope.

Before scaling to 4,400 tasks, I would make three changes:

1. **Represent shared flows and action gates.** Add workflow and artefact identifiers, dependencies, distinct current/future actors, approver role and approval validity. Include explicit exception paths, transferred work and new upkeep. Validate that each removed step is counted once across the flow.
2. **Separate occupational hypotheses from company facts.** Jev should distinguish step-level rule coverage, missing input, channel access and legal branches. API access, local test use, volumes and worker preferences stay unknown until intake. Benchmark practitioner-labelled steps with and without jev scores; agreement between models is not accuracy.
3. **Make every opportunity testable and sourced.** Add dated claim evidence through E4, opened-source status, legal jurisdiction and applicability, acceptance criteria and cost ranges. Derive arithmetic automatically; include negative savings and uncertainty scenarios. Pilot timers and a held-out set of messy cases must test both touch time and lawful decisions before fleet-wide recommendations.
