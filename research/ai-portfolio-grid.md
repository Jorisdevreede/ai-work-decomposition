# The AI Portfolio Grid (source notes)

Source: an X article by Alex Lieberman (@businessbarista), "What Actually Goes Into an AI Strategy, and
Where Do We Start?", by Matt Siegel, @armanhezarkhani and @businessbarista (Tenex Labs). Pasted by Joris
on 2026-10-16; no URL opened in this session, so cite it by title and authors only. The article's quadrant
graphic was an image and did not come through; the quadrant rules below are taken from the text.

## The grid

Every initiative, running or proposed, is scored on two questions:

- **Can we execute it today?** (readiness) Is the data available? Is the process defined well enough to
  measure? Is there an owner who wants it, and someone who can deploy and support it?
- **How big is the prize?** (impact) How much would it move a goal leadership has already set? How strong
  is the evidence? Would it unlock other work?

Quadrants named in the text:

| Quadrant | Readiness | Prize | Rule from the text |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accelerate Now | high | high | First wave. Rank by odds of success first, then by return. Prefer contained work with a willing owner. Keep 3 to 5 significant initiatives active. |
| Invest to Unlock | low | high | Usually the call **Edit**: fix what blocks it (owner, measure, data, policy). Enabling work that unblocks several big-prize initiatives belongs in the first wave even though it returns nothing directly. |
| Quick Wins | high | low | Cheap, owner, results within weeks: do it with a lighter check (time saved, and say where the time goes). Count separately from goal progress. Run alongside the first wave. |
| Deprioritize | low | low | Usually the call **Stop** or **Merge**. |

Security, compliance and vendor policy set the boundary for what can reach production; when they block
most of the grid, fixing them is one of the first initiatives.

## The calls

- **Continue:** owner, evidence it works, link to a current goal (or for a small fix: time saved that goes somewhere you can name).
- **Edit:** promising but missing an owner, a measure or a fix. Gets a deadline and a written test; stops if it misses them.
- **Merge:** two or more efforts do the same job; combine under the owner whose version works best.
- **Stop:** no owner, no evidence or no payoff.
- **Start:** for new ideas only, when they earn it (owner, checkable outcome, cost range).

## Rules for a pilot (Step 3)

- A business owner; a checkable outcome "improve [measure] from [baseline] to [target] by [date]"; a cost
  range (employee time, process change, data work, implementation, software).
- Hours saved count only when you say beforehand where they go (more output, faster turnaround, fewer
  errors, growth without hires, roles not backfilled) and who confirms it. "Gross productivity gain is not
  realized savings."
- Early evidence: faster/cheaper/better without quality loss, people use it daily, the AI caused the
  gain, the cost and benefit assumptions held. Don't annualise less than a quarter in production.
- Go/no-go written before the pilot: expand if ..., revise if ..., stop if .... A revision gets one more cycle.
- Readiness before production, all eight: value clear; process understood and measurable; data
  accessible; someone can deploy and support it; users will take part; the team has capacity to change;
  a business owner owns the result; legal controls and guardrails in place.

## Payback by kind of work (Step 4.5, the authors' rough figures)

Content generation 2 to 4 weeks; workflow automation on a defined process 2 to 4 months; predictive
analytics on integrated data 6 to 12 months; platform-level change 12 to 24 months.
