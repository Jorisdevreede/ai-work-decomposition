# 35-3 Food and Beverage Serving Workers

O*NET minor group 35-3: 2 occupations (bartenders, waiters and waitresses), 4 automate and amplify tasks (4 automate, 0 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

"Sales taxes" on the check is a US notion: in the Netherlands menu prices include btw and the receipt shows the 9% and 21% split.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Preparing checks, explaining dishes: Lightspeed Order Anywhere (Lightspeed Restaurant K-Series) lets guests scan a QR code, read item descriptions and allergen information, order and pay; orders go to bar or kitchen through the POS. Dutch is one of six languages [S1] E2.
- Preparing checks: Orderli (Rotterdam and Delft) offers QR ordering and payment with iDEAL and links to Lightspeed, unTill, Vectron and MplusKASSA [S2] E2.
- Planning menus, explaining dishes: Apicbase costs each recipe from ingredient prices, stores allergen and dietary data per recipe and sorts dishes in a profitability matrix [S3] E2.
- Creating drink recipes: Backbar's free calculator adds pour cost per ingredient and gives the menu price for a target pour-cost percentage [S4] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Lightspeed reports about 8% more transactions for restaurants using Order Anywhere, and Fitzbillies (UK) reports 20% higher spend per transaction for QR orders against counter orders. Vendor case [S1] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Prepare checks (1 of 4): configure. VAT codes per item in the POS, including the cocktail rule: 21% on the alcoholic part and 9% on the rest only if the drinks are also sold unmixed and the prices add up [S5]. QR ordering with pay-at-table eliminates the separate check step [S1][S2].
- Explain how dishes are prepared (1 of 4): a records app (recipe database with allergens [S3]) feeds the digital menu, deterministically. A bounded model step can answer a guest's free-text question in a conversation, but only from the recipe record. Check: the answer quotes the record, and any allergen question without a complete record goes to staff (E1).
- Create drink recipes, plan bar menus (2 of 4): drafting a new recipe or a menu line-up is a bounded model step. Checks: costing and margin in the recipe tool [S3][S4], allergen tags, the VAT rule, and a taste test by the bartender, which stays manual. Today pour costs often sit in spreadsheets; the recipe tool replaces them (E1).
- Agent: none.

## 4. Gates

- Allergens: businesses must tell guests about 14 allergens in unpacked food; spoken information is allowed if a notice says guests can ask [S6]. In 2022 only 41% of 9,407 inspected horeca businesses complied [S7]. A model that guesses ingredients is a food-safety risk.
- Tax: the Belastingdienst advises a reliable register, preferably with the Keurmerk Betrouwbaar Afrekensysteem, and a seven-year retention duty [S8].
- Bar menu pricing: municipalities can ban drink promotions; Amsterdam bans prices below 60% of the usual price for 24 hours or less since 1 April 2022 [S9].
- EU AI Act art. 50(1): a guest-facing chatbot must say it is an AI unless that is obvious [S10]; it applies from 2 August 2026 [S11].
- WOR art. 27(1)(l): per-waiter sales logs in the POS can count as a facility to monitor performance, so the works council must consent [S12].
