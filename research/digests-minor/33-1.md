# 33-1 Supervisors of Protective Service Workers

O*NET minor group 33-1: 4 occupations (first-line supervisors of correctional officers, police and detectives, firefighting and prevention workers, security workers), 13 automate and amplify tasks (10 automate, 3 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source. The list is desk work (paper, policy, rosters), not operational command.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Police reports: Axon Draft One drafts a narrative from body-camera audio; the officer reviews and signs, use is logged [S1] E2 (US).
- Recorded interviews: since summer 2025 the Dutch police tests speech-to-text plus large language models to summarise recorded interviews; results due mid-2026 [S2] E2 (test).
- Work schedules: ORTEC Workforce (Dutch) generates schedules with labour law and collective agreements as hard and soft constraints; planners keep final control [S3] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Anchorage Police Department ended a three-month Draft One trial: reviewing drafts ate the time saved; an independent study found "no real time-savings advantage" [S4] E3. Axon's own quality study (24 experts) is a vendor claim [S1].
- Dutch Innovatiewet pilot (Noord-Holland, Oost-Nederland): a short police report plus camera footage gave no net drop in police workload, because compiling the footage added work (evaluation September 2024) [S2] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Rosters, 1 task: configure a rules-based scheduler; working-time rules are plain constraints, no model (E1).
- Records, logs, maps and forms, 4 of 13 tasks: a records app with structured incident and shift forms and an artefact generator for standard reports. This replaces Word templates, spreadsheets and the duty mailbox (E1). A bounded model loop may draft a narrative from a recording; check: side-by-side transcript and the author signs [S1]. Measure review time, not draft time [S4].
- Offender information review, 1 task: rule-based flags in the records app (medical, separation, release dates). No model risk score (E1; see gates).
- Policies and procedures, 3 tasks, and press releases and correspondence, 1 task: an artefact generator with a bounded drafting step; check: a named owner approves; versions live in a document system, not mail (E1).
- Legal checks, 2 tasks (orders against the law; fire codes into permit procedures): a judgement step that quotes the article it relies on, e.g. PGS 15, which safety regions apply to permits [S5]; a lawyer or fire officer decides.
- Telling staff about rule changes, 1 task: notification plus an answer desk on the current rule texts (E1).
- Agent: not needed.

## 4. Gates

- AI Act art. 5 bans crime-risk prediction based solely on profiling, real-time remote biometric identification in public for police (narrow exceptions), and emotion recognition at work [S6]; in force since 2 February 2025 [S7]. Annex III lists law enforcement uses and worker management and monitoring as high-risk [S8], from 2 December 2027 [S7][S9].
- Wet politiegegevens art. 7a: no adverse decision based solely on automated processing without prior human intervention; no discriminatory profiling [S10].
- AVG: DPIA mandatory for systematic employee monitoring and for body-worn cameras (AP list items 10, 11) [S11]; art. 22 for staff decisions [S12].
- WOR: working and rest-time rules need works council consent (art. 27(1)(b)), staff monitoring too (27(1)(l)); public tasks themselves are not a council matter, their effects on staff work are (art. 46d(b)) [S13].
- Failures: Amnesty called the Roermond "Sensing" predictive policing project automated discrimination and mass surveillance [S14]. A body camera misses what the officer saw but did not say; King County prosecutors told officers not to write narratives with AI [S4].
