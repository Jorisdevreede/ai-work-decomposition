# 51-4 Metal Workers and Plastic Workers

O*NET minor group 51-4: 4 occupations (rolling machine, multiple machine tool, welding/soldering/brazing machine and plating machine setters, operators and tenders), 4 automate and amplify tasks (4 automate, 0 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

The list is the paperwork slice of shop-floor jobs: three of four tasks are production records, one is shop maths. Setting and tending the machines is not in it.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Recording production quantities and machine states: MPDV HYDRA X Machine Monitoring mApp collects produced quantities and machine statuses from the machine control or by manual entry and assigns them to the workplace [S1] E2.
- Recording welding data on production reports: Fronius WeldCube Premium records welding data per seam and per component, gives reports "at the touch of a button" and passes data to MES or ERP through a web API [S2] E2.
- Computing gear dimensions: KISSsoft (Gleason) Gears module calculates cylindrical, bevel, worm and other gear types to ISO, DIN, AGMA and VDI standards, with sizing tools [S3] E2.
- Computing machine settings: CAM systems and tool makers' cutting-data tools give feeds and speeds (E1, model knowledge; the vendor pages I tried did not load).

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Sintex, Denmark (magnetic systems, powder metal parts): operators collected production data by hand and managers compiled it in Excel. Factbird read live PLC data on 10 machines, later 30. Efficiency rose 5% in the first week. Vendor case [S4] E3.
- Jay Industries, Ohio (metal fabrication): Plex MES gave 6% higher OEE, 8% higher machine availability, 86% less inventory variance and $152,000 a year in savings. Vendor case [S5] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Record mill production, record operational information, maintain production records (3 of 4 tasks): deterministic. An integration from the PLC into a records app (MES or the ERP shop-floor module) and a dashboard replace the schedule sheet. Today the glue is a paper sheet per shift and a supervisor's spreadsheet, as at Sintex [S4]. Operators only add reason codes for stops and scrap.
- Compute gear dimensions or machine settings (1 of 4): configure. Standard formulas live in gear software [S3], CNC cycles and CAM tool libraries. No model needed.
- Bounded model step: only for the free text around the numbers, such as a shift handover drafted from the counts and the operator's notes. Check: every number in the draft must match the records app (E1).
- Agent: none. Stays manual: setting up, judging a weld or a coating, signing quality records.

## 4. Gates

- WOR art. 27(1)(l): machine data tied to an operator login is a facility suited to observing attendance, behaviour or performance, so the works council must consent; 27(1)(k) covers processing personal data [S6].
- AVG: operator-linked counts are personal data; plan a DPIA if they feed performance reviews (E1, model knowledge).
- EU AI Act: AI that monitors and evaluates workers is high-risk under Annex III 4(b) [S7]. These rules apply from 2 December 2027 under the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 [S8]. Plain counting is not AI. Systems that infer workers' emotions are prohibited, except for medical or safety reasons, art. 5(1)(f) [S9].
- Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 applies from 20 January 2027 and covers AI-powered safety functions [S10]. The Omnibus adds AI safety requirements by delegated acts that apply by 2 August 2028 [S8]. This matters if a model steers the machine, not if it writes reports.
