# 53-4 Rail transport workers

DIGEST-C, 2026-10-15. 4 automate/amplify tasks across 3 occupations (locomotive engineers, railroad conductors and yardmasters, subway and streetcar operators). Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

The work is driving and shunting trains. The automatable part is paperwork: incident, delay and shift reports, and reading schedules, switching orders and waybills to plan yard work.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **CREWS (SISCOG, Portugal)**: assigns daily work to more than 20,000 drivers and guards in several European countries [S1]. A version for NS, CREWS_NS, was published in AI Magazine in 1998 [S2]. Crew rostering, not report writing, but it is where drivers' duties come from. E2

No product found that drafts rail incident reports; the regulator's digital form is the intake (see Gates).

## 2. Cases with numbers

None found. Searched for published results from NS, ProRail and SISCOG on crew planning, and for digital incident or delay reporting at Dutch rail operators.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **"Document and prepare reports of accidents, unscheduled stops, or delays"** and **"complete reports, including shift summaries"**: train number, times, location and delay minutes already sit in train-running data (E1). An integration prefills the report; a shift summary is generated, not written. Rung: deterministic.
- **The driver's account**: a bounded model step turns a dictated or typed note into the report's fields (what happened, cause, action). Check: required fields filled, times and places match the train log. Stop: the driver or controller confirms; nothing goes to the regulator without a person.
- **"Review schedules, switching orders, way bills, and shipping records"**: an integration of consignment data into a yard work list, sorted by track and departure. Deterministic; the yardmaster plans.
- Glue today (E1): phone calls to traffic control and paper or mailed forms between driver, operator and safety department.

## 4. Gates

- **ILT reporting**: every incident or accident on Dutch rail, including metro and tram, is reported to the ILT by phone. Companies file a digital special-occurrence report (MBV) within 72 hours (24 hours for a signal passed at danger). A preliminary MBV is allowed; the full report follows within 7 days. The ILT passes every rail incident to the Dutch Safety Board (OVV) [S3].
- **EU AI Act**: annex III point 2 covers road traffic, digital infrastructure, water, gas, heating and electricity, not rail [S4]. AI as a rail safety component falls under annex I through the rail interoperability directive 2016/797 [S5]. A report drafter is neither. AI that allocates duties based on individual behaviour or monitors drivers' performance is annex III point 4(b) [S4].
- **WOR article 27(1)(l)**: cab recorders or apps used to watch drivers' behaviour or performance are staff tracking systems and need works council consent [S6].
- **Working time**: rosters must respect the Arbeidstijdenwet and its transport decree (E1); encode these as hard rules, not model output.
