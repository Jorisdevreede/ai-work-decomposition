# 43-1 First-Line Supervisors of Office and Administrative Support Workers

O*NET minor group 43-1: 1 occupation (First-Line Supervisors of Office and Administrative Support Workers), 10 automate and amplify tasks (7 automate, 3 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Work schedules and duty assignments: Dyflexis generates three schedule options from availability, contracts, preferences and skills; the planner picks and edits one [S1] E2.
- Schedules by budget and workload: Quinyx Forecasting turns sales, footfall, promotions and opening hours into staffing demand; "up to 97%" accuracy is a vendor claim [S2] E2.
- Compute commissions: Qobra calculates commission plans from CRM data and gives each employee a statement per period [S3] E2.
- Policy and procedure questions: TOPdesk AI Answer Assistant drafts ticket answers and turns solved tickets into draft knowledge items; the team approves what goes out [S4] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Commissions, vendor case: DataSnipper cut commission processing from 14 to 18 days to 1 day; GoCardless 3 to 4 days to 1. Customer quotes on Qobra's site [S3] E3.
- Reports and correspondence: over 20,000 UK civil servants using mainly Microsoft 365 Copilot for 3 months reported saving 26 minutes a day on drafting, summarising, records and reports. Self-reported [S5] E3.
- Customer questions: an AI assistant raised issues resolved per hour by about 15% across 5,172 support agents, most for less experienced staff. Measured by researchers in a support centre [S6] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Compute balances, totals, commissions (1 of 10 tasks): deterministic, rules in the ERP or a commission tool, not a spreadsheet mailed to finance (E1).
- Schedules (2 of 10): configure a workforce tool with contract hours, skills and labour-law rules; the solver proposes, the supervisor approves. Leave and swap requests now travel by mail into Excel; a records app with self-service swaps replaces that (E1).
- Maintain and review records (2 of 10): records app plus a dashboard with exception rules. A bounded model loop only reads free-text notes, checked against the record (E1).
- Reports, procedures and policies (2 of 10): artefact generator. Figures by query, model-drafted text, the supervisor checks each number and signs (E1).
- Complaints and policy questions (1 of 10): answer desk on the approved knowledge base, deterministic plus a bounded model loop: the draft cites its source, a person sends. Disputes stay manual (E1).
- Budget input and training design (2 of 10): dashboard, then a judgement step (E1). No task needs an agent.

## 4. Gates

- AVG art. 22: no decision based solely on automated processing with legal or similarly significant effect; where allowed, a right to human intervention and to contest [S7]. Rosters that cut hours need a real human decision.
- DPIA: required for systematic automated evaluation, including profiling, behind significant decisions [S8].
- EU AI Act Annex III 4(b): AI that allocates tasks on individual behaviour or traits, or monitors and evaluates workers, is high-risk [S9]. Annex III duties apply from 2 December 2027 under the AI Omnibus, in force since 27 July 2026; transparency rules from August 2026 [S10]. Art. 5(1)(f) bans inferring emotions at work [S11].
- WOR art. 27(1)(b) and (l): works council consent for work and rest time schemes and for facilities able to check presence, behaviour or performance [S12]. A dashboard on individual output falls under (l) (E1).
- Arbeidstijdenwet art. 4:2: where no other term applies, the schedule goes out at least 28 days ahead [S13].
