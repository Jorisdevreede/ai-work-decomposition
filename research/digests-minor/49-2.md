# 49-2 Electrical and electronic equipment installers and repairers

DIGEST-C, 2026-10-15. 10 automate/amplify tasks across 8 occupations (office machine and ATM repairers, telecom and tower installers, avionics technicians, transport and industrial electronics repairers, audiovisual and alarm installers). Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

Repair is hands-on; the automatable part is the record: work orders, service logs, invoices, sign-offs.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **FieldBuddy (Netherlands)**: work orders that fill themselves in, checklists for technicians, automatic conversion of a work order into an invoice, asset management, and "FieldBuddy AI" for job assignment and scheduling. For "repair bills ... time cards" and "invoices or warranties". [S1] E2
- **Dynamics 365 Field Service with Copilot (Microsoft)**: drafts a work order in Outlook from a customer email and writes recaps of work orders for managers. Joulz (Netherlands) is quoted; no result given. [S2] E2
- **AMOS Production (Swiss-AS, Switzerland)**: digital completion of work orders stored as PDF/A, integrated e-signature, real-time recording at the aircraft (AMOSmobile/EXEC), parts requests. For avionics "keep records of maintenance and repair work". [S3] E2

## 2. Cases with numbers

- **easyJet (IATA presentation, undated)**: upgraded AMOS and added e-signature at all its maintenance stations. Reported 80% less paper, paperwork processing cut "circa 80%", and fewer contract staff for records checks. Self-reported by the airline, no baseline given. [S4] E3

No measured case found for field service work orders.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Repair bills, time cards, invoices, warranties**: an artefact generator on the work order (hours, parts, rates, serial number). Rung: configure the field service tool. Today a photographed work order is mailed to the office and retyped into the ERP (E1).
- **Service records, equipment logs, calibrations, test reports**: a records app per installed asset with fixed checklists; calibration and test due dates raise notifications.
- **"Perform database verifications"**: a reconciliation script between the inventory database and what the technician found, with explicit match rules. A model only reads free-text exceptions.
- **Technician notes**: a bounded model step turns a dictated note into fields (fault, cause, action, parts). Check: required fields filled, part numbers exist in the catalogue, hours match the clock. Stop: the technician confirms; after one failed retry, manual entry.
- **Project status reports**: a dashboard of work order states; a model may draft the weekly text. Check: every number traces to the dashboard. Stop: the project lead sends.
- **"Sign overhaul documents"**: stays with a person. Software collects the evidence and blocks signing until checks pass.

## 4. Gates

- **Aviation**: release to service is signed by authorised certifying staff (E1). easyJet needed its competent authority to approve the e-signature, and lists authority buy-in as a lesson [S4]. In the Netherlands that authority is the ILT (E1).
- **WOR article 27(1)(l)**: workflow systems and GPS or on-board computers in vans are staff tracking systems; without works council consent they may in principle not be used [S5].
- **GDPR**: a DPIA is mandatory for systematic monitoring of employees, including GPS in their vehicles (AP list, item 11) [S6].
- **EU AI Act**: AI that allocates jobs based on individual behaviour or traits, or monitors performance, is high-risk under annex III point 4(b) [S7]; this can catch AI dispatch features. These duties apply from 2 December 2027 [S8]. Scheduling on skills, distance and availability rules alone is not AI (E1).
- **Fire alarm installers**: maintenance and the logbook follow NEN 2654 (E1).
- **Reported failure**: easyJet advises not combining a system upgrade with e-signature [S4].
