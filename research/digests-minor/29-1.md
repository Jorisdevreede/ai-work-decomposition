# 29-1 Healthcare Diagnosing or Treating Practitioners

O*NET minor group 29-1: 48 occupations (e.g. physicians, nurses, pharmacists, therapists), 161 automate and amplify tasks (80 automate, 81 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

Reading O*NET: a few tasks are US-specific (Medicaid, state rules).

## 1. Products that do this today

- Records: Autoscriber (Eindhoven) records the consultation; the clinician validates; the note goes into HiX, Nedap ONS or Nexus. It claims 15-20 minutes saved per consult, no method given [S1] E2. ChipSoft lists it, Juvoly and Delphyr as HiX partners [S2].
- Letters and coding: Juvoly ("Powered by Tandem") drafts referral letters and suggests ICPC, ICD-10 and SNOMED codes [S3]; Tandem's scribe and coding carry MDR class IIa [S4] E2.
- Record questions: Delphyr (Amsterdam) answers clinicians' questions on the record; MDR certification "in progress" [S5] E2.
- Medication review: the G-Standaard checks interactions, contraindications, doses and duplicates; KNMP sets the content [S6] E2.
- Image reading: Transpara (ScreenPoint Medical BV, Dutch) flags breast cancer on mammograms [S7] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Documentation: Capio Ramsay Santé (Sweden), 1,295 clinicians, 236,153 Tandem notes: self-estimated time per note fell from 6.69 to 4.72 minutes (29%); median editing stayed 93 seconds. Peer-reviewed, three of seven authors from the vendor [S8] E3.
- Screening: MASAI RCT (Sweden, 105,000+ women, Transpara): 12% fewer interval cancers, sensitivity 80.5% against 73.8% at equal specificity (vendor summary of the Lancet paper) [S7] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Records and documentation (about 54 of 161 tasks): deterministic plus a bounded model loop.** Vitals, results, codes and medication arrive by integration. A scribe drafts the narrative; the clinician validates before write-back [S1]. Check: logged edit time and a monthly sample audit for omissions. Dictation queues and letters by e-mail or fax give way to structured exchange (E1).
- **Prescribing and medication review (about 14): deterministic.** Rule-based surveillance exists [S6]; a model only reads free-text indications. Prescribing trends: a dashboard.
- **Patient and public education (about 30): answer desk** drafting only from vetted texts such as Thuisarts (NHG, FMS and Patiëntenfederatie, guideline-based) [S9]. Check: each answer cites its paragraph; personal advice gets clinician sign-off.
- **Tests and diagnosis (about 24):** calculations such as valve area are plain code (E1); image reading is a certified judgement step beside a radiologist [S7].
- **Literature, protocols, research (about 40): artefact generator** with citations, committee-approved.
- No task needs an agent (E1).

## 4. Gates

- **IGJ (February 2025):** providers are legally responsible for careful use of generative AI: know whether it is a medical device, assess risks, train, test, monitor after updates; it names medication errors from wrong registration [S10].
- **MDR and AI Act:** decision software is usually MDR class IIa or higher (E1). MDR is in AI Act Annex I [S11]; such AI needing a notified body is high-risk [S12], from 2 August 2028; Annex III duties from 2 December 2027 [S13]. Annex III covers public eligibility for care (5(a)) and emergency triage (5(d)) [S14]. Patient chatbots must disclose AI from 2 August 2026 [S15].
- **WGBO:** the dossier is kept twenty years after the last change [S16].
- **Wkkgz:** calamities go to the IGJ without delay [S17].
- **Wet BIG:** a prescription comes from a named practitioner under Wet BIG art. 36(14) [S18]; AI drafts, it does not prescribe.
- **AVG:** health data are special category; expect a DPIA (E1); solely automated care decisions need an art. 22(2) basis [S19].
- **Works council:** ambient recording captures staff performance: WOR art. 27(1)(l) [S20].
