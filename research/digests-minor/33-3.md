# 33-3 Law Enforcement Workers

O*NET minor group 33-3: 8 occupations (e.g. police patrol officers, detectives, intelligence analysts, correctional officers, parking enforcement, customs officers), 27 automate and amplify tasks (9 automate, 18 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

A US public-sector bundle; 15 of 27 tasks are the intelligence analyst's.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Drafting, summarising, translating: PolitieGPT, an open-source model on police servers with retrieval over internal policy; not for Wpg police data [S1] E2.
- Patrol report from bodycam audio: Axon Draft One (US); officers clear flagged passages and confirm [S2] E2.
- Gaps in a report: Politie Slimme Keuzehulp asks fraud victims for missing facts, with argumentation logic, not an LLM [S3] E2.
- Records triage: Politie BEAR tags sentences by topic and orders documents; summarising was rejected because details matter [S4] E2.
- Link charts, timelines, network analysis: i2 Analyst's Notebook [S5] E2.
- Identifying suspects: CATCH (Idemia) compares faces with the criminal records database; two independent experts decide [S6] E2.
- Parking records: Maastricht's scan car checks plates against the Nationaal Parkeer Register; an officer checks every photo [S7] E2.
- Duties: TARIC holds EU duty measures, sent daily to national customs [S8] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Draft One randomised trial, New Hampshire (Adams et al., Journal of Experimental Criminology): reports were not written faster. Fort Collins police measured 8 against 23 minutes, a department figure [S2] E3.
- LMIO: 42,000 trade-fraud reports a year, about 10,000 no offence after review; the Keuzehulp targets those (2019) [S9] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Records, logs, databases, duties, visitor advice (5 of 27):** configure. Records apps; duty is a TARIC lookup plus national rates [S8]; an answer desk for visitors (E1).
- **Search, links, money flows, trends (8 of 27):** integration and dashboard. Entity matching and queries are plain code; a bounded model may tag free text, as BEAR does, while the analyst reads the source [S4]. Glue today: spreadsheet exports from BVH and telecom data loaded by hand (E1).
- **Gap checks (2 of 27):** rules first [S3]; a bounded model reads against a checklist, the detective decides (E1).
- **Report drafting (4 of 27):** artefact generator, officer signs; do not count on time saved [S2].
- **Translation (1 of 27):** bounded model for triage, sworn interpreter for evidence (E1).
- **Contested citations (1 of 27):** workflow with a judgement step [S7].
- **Profiles, predictions, tactics, case issues, threat study, field intelligence (6 of 27):** stays manual or judgement step. No agent.

## 4. Gates

- **Wpg:** Solely automated decisions with adverse effect need prior human intervention (art. 7a); DPIA for high-risk processing (art. 4c); daily-task data at most five years (art. 8) [S10].
- **AI Act:** offence prediction solely from profiling is banned, art. 5(1)(d); real-time remote biometric identification in public needs prior authorisation, art. 5(1)(h) [S11]. Annex III 6 (evidence reliability, reoffending risk, profiling) and 7 (border) are high-risk [S12]. Bans since 2 February 2025, Annex III from 2 December 2027 [S13]. Art. 50 exempts crime detection, except public reporting channels [S14].
- **Withdrawn and criticised:** the CAS crime forecast was phased out on 1 December 2025 after an evaluation [S15]. Amnesty's 2020 report calls Roermond's Sensing project automated discrimination and mass surveillance [S16]. The EFF warns officers could blame Draft One for a false statement [S2].
- **Works council:** WOR 27(1)(l) consent on staff monitoring [S17]. Politie's rule-based Protective Monitoring has works council members in its review group [S18].
