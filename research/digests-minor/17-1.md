# 17-1 Architects, Surveyors, and Cartographers

O*NET minor group 17-1: 5 occupations (architects, landscape architects, cartographers and photogrammetrists, surveyors, geodetic surveyors), 28 automate and amplify tasks (8 automate, 20 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source. In the Netherlands much of this work sits with Kadaster, a public body (E1).

## 1. Products that do this today

- Design information and specifications: Nemetschek's AI Assistant in Archicad answers queries against the BIM model in chat; the Allplan version also searches the web [S1] E2.
- Checking drawings and specifications: Solibri (Nemetschek) does rule-based BIM model checking, clash detection and quantity take-off [S2] E2.
- Analysing aerial and satellite images: Esri ArcGIS offers over 100 pretrained models, e.g. building footprints and land cover [S3] E2.
- Verifying survey data: Trimble Business Center runs least-squares adjustment, helps correct field blunders, writes adjustment reports [S4] E2.
- Compiling map data: Cyclomedia (Zaltbommel) captures street-level imagery and LiDAR and detects pavement defects with AI [S5] E2.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Kadaster handles about one million deeds a year. It runs OCR on handwritten deeds back to 1832, AI on millions of field sketches, and object detection (storage tanks, greenhouses) for the topographic map. People confirm results. No measured time saving; a deed archive search "can soon" cut days to hours [S6] E3.
- Kadaster's deed data extraction took five years to reach human-level performance and must meet a 99.5% quality bar, so it chains three models [S7] E3.
- Geronimo.AI's ORCA prototype classifies map errors from oblique aerial photos for the BGT, BAG and WOZ, still updated largely by hand. No results yet [S8] E3.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Energy savings (1 task): deterministic. BENG has applied to all new buildings since 1 January 2021, calculated under NTA 8800 in BRL 9501-attested software [S9]. A model may explain results, not compute them (E1).
- Databases, verification, distribution, map revision (10 tasks): deterministic. Adjustment and QA rules [S4], PDOK services for distribution (E1). Map updating adds a bounded model loop: detection proposes, a topographer confirms against the quality bar [S6][S7]. TOP10NL already renews a region five times a year from aerial and panoramic photos [S10].
- Documents (9 tasks: contract documents, specifications, manuals, proposals, reports): artefact generator fed by the BIM model and specification library; a model drafts, rules check [S2], the architect signs (E1).
- Design and site analysis (7 tasks): simulation is deterministic; the design stays with the designer (E1).
- Research (2 tasks): answer desk with citations (E1).
- Glue today: quantity lists in Excel, drawings and issue lists by mail (E1). Replace with a common data environment and model-based issue tracking. No agent needed.

## 4. Gates

- AI Act: no Annex III area covers design, surveying or mapping [S11]; those duties start 2 December 2027 anyway [S12]. Art. 50 marking of generated images applies from 2 August 2026 [S13].
- Public registers: Kadaster lists Akte AI in the national algorithm register as in use, minimal risk, human in the loop [S14].
- AVG: imagery shows people and homes. Kadaster publishes detected objects, not the images [S6]. Cadastral data links owners to parcels: personal data, DPIA first (E1).
- Titles: "architect" and "tuin- en landschapsarchitect" are protected; over 14,000 are registered [S15]. Responsibility stays with them (E1).
- Energy calculations need a certified adviser and attested software [S9].
- WOR art. 27(1)(l): tools that log drafters' work need works-council consent [S16].
- Risk: OCR still errs, so Kadaster uses it only as support [S6].
