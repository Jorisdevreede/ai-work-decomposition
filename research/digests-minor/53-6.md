# 53-6 Other Transportation Workers

O*NET minor group 53-6: 7 occupations (e.g. bridge and lock tenders, traffic technicians, transportation and aviation inspectors, passenger attendants), 12 automate and amplify tasks (11 automate, 1 amplify). DIGEST-B, 2026-10-15. Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source. The bridge tender's core work, opening the bridge, is not on the list.

## 1. Products that do this today

- Vehicle log review: Roadsoft (Dutch) announced an AI assistant that takes a tachograph infringement, contacts the driver by phone or WhatsApp and files an auditable dossier [S1] E2.
- Remote bridge and lock operation: Rijkswaterstaat control rooms use cameras, VHF, AIS and radar; one team runs 45 objects over 110 km [S2] E2.
- Signal timing: Velsen's iVRI (Royal HaskoningDHV) sets green phases from loops, floating car data, weather, trains and bridges; the municipality sets the parameters [S3] E2. The Netherlands has 1,450 such lights [S4].
- Maintenance records: Lufthansa Technik AVIATAR Technical Repetitives Examination reads logbook write-ups, matches differently worded components and assigns ATA chapters, at more than 20 airlines [S5] E2. It finds repeat defects, not missed intervals.

## 2. Cases with numbers

- Rijkswaterstaat Brabant: moving bridge and lock control to Tilburg took operating staff from 160 to just over 50 (press report; remote operation, no AI) [S6] E3.
- No AI case with a measured result found.

## 3. Deterministic first

- Reports and compiled data, 5 of 12 tasks (accident, fuel sales and freight reports, charts, count data): deterministic, a records app plus artefact generator. Sales come from the till, counts from loops and cameras, so hand count sheets and the mailed spreadsheet go (E1).
- Calculations, 2 tasks (signal timings, tonnage and stability): deterministic, certified software, no model. Some inland stowage programmes do not match the stability manual; the boatmaster stays responsible [S7].
- Record review, 2 tasks (maintenance intervals, vehicle logs): tachograph files are structured, so driving-time rules run as plain code (E1). A bounded model loop only reads scanned logbooks or free-text write-ups; check: each entry links to its source page and a certifying person signs. Driver follow-up by mail and phone becomes workflow automation.
- Information, 2 tasks (roads; routes, gates, fares, timetables): an answer desk on live timetable data (E1).
- Service reminders: records app plus notification (E1).
- Agent: not needed. Opening a bridge stays manual; detection only alerts.

## 4. Gates

- AI Act: Annex III point 2 covers safety components in road traffic management [S8]; high-risk duties apply from 2 December 2027, Annex I products (civil aviation, vehicles, marine equipment) from 2 August 2028 [S9][S10]. Velsen registers its iVRI as "impactful", not high-risk [S3]. Art. 5 applies since 2 February 2025; a passenger chatbot must say it is AI (art. 50) since 2 August 2026 [S9].
- AVG: DPIA mandatory for systematic employee monitoring, location data and cameras on public space [S11]. No driver sanction based solely on automated processing (art. 22) [S12].
- WOR 27(1)(l): tachograph analysis and control-room cameras that also watch staff need works council consent [S13].
- Tachograph: download the vehicle unit every 90 days, driver cards every 28, keep a year; ILT enforces [S14].
- Bad input: ILT found manipulation in 1 in 6 trucks at targeted inspections [S15]. A clean log proves little.
- Failures: at two remote Zaandam bridges people on the moving deck were not seen on camera (a death in 2015, two injured in 2018); the OVV found 72 shortcomings and no national camera standard [S16]. The operator was convicted [S17].
