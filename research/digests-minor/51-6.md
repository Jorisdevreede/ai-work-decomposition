# 51-6 Textile machine operators and patternmakers

DIGEST-C, 2026-10-15. 2 automate/amplify tasks across 2 occupations (textile knitting and weaving machine operators, fabric and apparel patternmakers). Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

This is hands-on work. Only the record keeping and the marker layout are in scope.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **Valia Furniture (Lectra, France)**: automatic processing of orders, marker generation and spread plans, with real-time production and maintenance data on dashboards. For "determine the best layout of pattern pieces to minimize waste" and "record information about work completed". [S1] E2
- **Valia Fashion (Lectra)**: order-to-cut workflow and dashboards for cutting equipment and sustainability KPIs, working with spreaders and cutters from any vendor. [S2] E2

## 2. Cases with numbers

- **Export City, Bosnia and Herzegovina, 2026**: an upholstery maker (about 400 staff, 1,500 pieces a day) moved to Valia Furniture with standard models and automatic job distribution; "around 9% material savings". Vendor case, no baseline or period given. [S3] E3

## 3. Deterministic first

"Determine the best layout of pattern pieces to minimize waste" is a classic optimisation problem. Nesting software solves it with fixed rules (grain line, fabric width, stripe matching, shrinkage) and a solver; configure, no language model needed. The patternmaker sets the rules and checks the marker before cutting. Marking odd fabrics and matching checks by eye stay manual.

"Record information about work completed and machine settings": knitting and weaving machines already hold counts, speeds and stops. Read them into an MES or a simple records app (eliminate the paper job card); a person adds only the reason for a stop. The glue today is paper job cards and a shift spreadsheet.

No bounded model step is needed for either task.

## 4. Gates

- **EU AI Act**: no annex III point applies; nesting and production records are not on the list (E1).
- **WOR art. 27(1)(l)**: machine logs tied to an operator can measure performance, so the works council must consent [S4].
- **GDPR**: only where records name operators (E1).
