# Digest 39-3: Entertainment Attendants and Related Workers

DIGEST-A, 2026-10-15. 8 automate/amplify O*NET tasks across 5 occupations (gambling dealers, sports book writers and runners, projectionists, amusement attendants, costume attendants).
Grades: E1 model knowledge, E2 a named product does this today, E3 a documented case with a source.

## 1. Products that do this today

- **Convious (Amsterdam), attraction ticketing:** one back end for web shop, gate till and kiosks, with live reporting across channels [S1]. Vendor says up to 65% of guests self-serve; Zoo Osnabrück says about half its visitors use the kiosks [S1]. For recording attendance, sales and receipts. E2.
- **Casino table systems:** card-reading shoes and electronic tables that settle hands and count drop exist; no product page was opened in this run. E1.

## 2. Cases with numbers

None found. Searched for measured results of casino collection-report automation, attraction ticketing in the Netherlands and costume-department tools; only vendor testimonials without a period turned up [S1].

## 3. Deterministic first

- **Collection reports (2 tasks):** deterministic code. Count sheets per table go into a form; code totals them, checks against the drop and chip float, flags variances and sends the report to the supervisor. No model. E1.
- **Compare hands (1 task):** deterministic code; the game rules are fixed. The dealer handles disputes. E1.
- **Rules and policy questions (2 tasks, with people):** answer desk on the house rules, on a screen or printed. A bounded model may match a free-text question to a rule and quote it. Stop: anything about losses, limits or stopping goes to staff, because the operator must record those signals [S2]. E1.
- **Attendance, sales, reservations, repairs (1 task):** configure the ticketing system for sales and attendance; repairs go in a records app. E1.
- **Projection reports and logs (1 task):** records app; attendance from ticketing and show logs from the cinema server fill most fields. E1.
- **Costume worksheets (1 task):** records app with lists per scene and actor. A bounded model may draft a dressing list from the script; code checks names against the cast list. E1.
- **Glue:** paper count sheets and Excel tallies mailed to supervisors become one form that feeds the report. Agent: none.

## 4. Gates

- **Wet op de kansspelen (Ksa):** casinos and game halls must train staff to recognise addiction, record signs of excessive play and check every player in Cruks before play [S2]. Quarterly reports on advertising and addiction prevention are due within 28 days of quarter end, in Excel or CSV [S3]. The Ksa supervises the Wwft (anti money laundering) for operators [S4].
- **GDPR:** addiction signals and Cruks results are personal data; keep them out of general chat logs (E1).
- **EU AI Act:** no Annex III area applies [S5]. A rules chatbot must tell players it is AI [S6].
- **WOR article 27:** consent needed if table systems measure dealer speed or errors [S7].
- **Reported failures and risks:** a Canadian tribunal held Air Canada liable for its chatbot's wrong fare advice (CA$812, February 2024) [S8]. A rules bot that misstates a payout carries the same kind of risk (E1).
