# North Star workshop: digest for mockup builders

Sources: Amplitude's *North Star Playbook* ("PB p.N"), Amplitude's *How-to Guide: Running Your North Star Workshop* 2024 ("G p.N"), and the Miro boards uXjVLUrsbXA= and uXjVEZwdygs= (both read 2026-10-15). Page numbers are the PDF pages.

## (a) The framework in brief

- **North Star Metric (NSM):** the one metric that "best captures the value customers derive from your product" (PB p.6). It is a rate, count or ratio and a leading indicator of business results (PB p.8). It is an outcome, so "Never try to influence the North Star directly" (PB p.9).
- **Inputs:** "a small set of influential, complementary factors" that drive the NSM (PB p.8). Teams can move them in their daily work (PB p.9). A common pattern is breadth, depth, frequency and efficiency (G p.18).
- **The work:** daily tasks aligned to the inputs (PB p.10), later framed as "bets" at levels 0–3 (PB p.51).
- **Checks** (PB p.14; G p.11): customer value, vision and strategy, leading indicator, actionable, understandable to non-technical partners, measurable, not vanity. Naming template: "Our North Star Metric is called X, which we define as Y" (PB p.24).

## (b) Workshop modules

The Playbook has no agenda of its own. It points to the Guide instead (PB p.22, 54, 62). The modules below follow the Guide's "Typical agenda for two hour North Star workshop" (G p.5).

**Participants:** one unbiased facilitator, and people from engineering, finance, design, product, analytics, sales, marketing and customer success, mixing "messy and analytical thinkers" (G p.6). More than "15-20 people" gets hard (G p.5).

| # | Module | Duration (G p.5) | Purpose | Steps as given | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening discussion | 15 min | Shared understanding (G p.7) | Discuss prompts on impact, strategy clarity, being product-led, and how product spend links to financial results (G p.7) | Not specified |
| 2 | Identify the game | 15 min | Pick the attention, transaction or productivity game (G p.8) | Ask "Which game is our business playing?"; pick one; tie it to the value exchange (G p.8–10) | The chosen game |
| 3 | Good and bad North Star | 15 min | Review the checklist and spot a bad NSM (G p.11) | Apply the checklist to your product; brainstorm "a terrible North Star" (G p.11) | Anti-examples as guardrails |
| 4 | Abstract exercise (optional) | 15 min | "free, organic, and lateral thinking" (G p.12) | Pick one or two toolkit exercises: Magic chart, Confidence ladder, Value moments (G p.12–13) | The exercise board |
| 5 | Statement exercise | 15 min | Words before numbers (G p.14) | Fill in the worksheet top-down: statement, inputs, opportunities, interventions, each with measurement options (G p.14–16; PB p.19–21) | A statement worksheet |
| 6 | Structure of NSM and inputs | "15 main" [sic] | See the NSM as a formula (G p.17) | Define the "handful of factors" (G p.17–18) | NSM = f(inputs) |
| 7 | North Star for another product | 15 min | Warm up "without the pressure" (G p.19) | In small groups, take OpenTable: find its game, its NSM and its inputs, then share (G p.19) | A worksheet for that product |
| 8 | Draft your own and converge | 30 min | Each person proposes a candidate; the group converges and documents (G p.24) | Brainstorm silently for "five to ten minutes"; pair and share; share with the group; test against the checklist (G p.24); use the "I would be more confident…" template (G p.25); answer four convergence questions: the game, the strongest candidates, what to eliminate, what information is missing (G p.26) | Documented decisions |

**After the workshop** (G p.27): define the name and definition (PB p.24–25), mind-map the inputs, then run the Greenfield test ("in two minutes") and the roadmap check (PB p.27–32), share the work (PB p.33), surface beliefs (PB p.35–36), and link work to inputs through bets (PB p.51–53).

**Where the sources disagree:** the Playbook says "less than two hours" (PB p.10); the Guide says "one to two hours" (G p.4) and a "two hour" agenda (G p.5) whose parts sum to 135 minutes. Module 7 is "Identify…" on G p.5 but "Build…" on G p.19.

**Zone 1: "North Star Framework Calisthenics".** From John Cutler's "North Star Workshop Lite" session, with an overview of Amplitude's Weekly Learning Users tree and five exercises:
- **A. Strategy influencers:** prompts such as "Unlike our competitors we…".
- **B. Try on Different NSMs:** four candidates in "a couple minutes".
- **C. North Star as Function:** "[NSM] is a function of our ability to do three things".
- **D. Bet Up:** start from the current bets and work "up" two levels of inputs.

## (d) Our proposal: an HR team becoming AI native

1. **Opening:** "Where does HR time go, and what would we do with it back?"
2. **Game:** mostly productivity; success can mean less time spent with HR.
3. **Good and bad:** vanity anti-examples such as prompts sent or licences issued.
4. **Abstract exercise:** a Confidence Ladder over the employee journey, marking where people would trust AI.
5. **Statement exercise:** pre-fill the input slots with AI levers: self-service, agent handling and data quality.
6. **Structure:** label the input slots breadth, depth, frequency and efficiency.
7. **Another product:** use the IT helpdesk instead of OpenTable.
8. **Converge:** silent drafting, pairing, dot votes against the seven checks, then the four convergence questions.
9. **After:** each AI pilot is a bet tied to one input.

**Illustrative only, not validated:**
- **NSM: Requests Resolved Right First Time.** The share of HR requests from employees and managers that are resolved correctly within one working day, with no follow-up ticket. Measured weekly.
- **Inputs:**
  - **Breadth:** the share of request types that have an AI-assisted path.
  - **Depth:** the share of requests that an AI agent completes end to end.
  - **Frequency:** the number of HR staff who use AI in their workflow each week.
  - **Efficiency:** the accuracy of AI answers in a weekly audited sample.

### Our proposal: eight team modules, in order

1. **Pick the game (17).** Agree what value HR delivers, so AI work aims at value, not activity.
2. **Metrics inventory (7, with 1).** List today's measures and flag vanity ones; the baseline for AI change.
3. **Strategic assumptions (4).** Make private beliefs, fears and hype about AI visible.
4. **Confidence ladder (2, with 3, 5).** Rate employee-journey steps; mark where AI could act and what signals trust.
5. **Statement (6, with 9).** Words before numbers for the AI-native team; an AI guide proposes wording.
6. **Converge (8, with 11, 15).** One NSM and its inputs, scored on the checklist; AI is an input, not the goal.
7. **Bet up (13).** Trace each AI pilot up to an input; stop those that trace to nothing.
8. **Context to intervention (10).** Turn inputs into time-bound AI interventions with an OKR.
