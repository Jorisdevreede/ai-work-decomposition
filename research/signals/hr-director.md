# Signals: HR director

Window: 16 July 2026 to 14 October 2026. Written 14 October 2026 by SCOUT.
Each link below was opened in this session. Grades: E2 a named product does this today; E3 a documented case or a legal source with a page you can read. Nothing here is E4. Vendor and marketing pieces are marked.

What shows up for this role: the decision is how far agents may go, who stays accountable when they are wrong, and which Dutch and EU duties already apply. The one employer that publishes both a failure and a later recovery is IBM, writing about itself, and the failure came from removing the human route before the tool was ready. This week's vendor announcements describe controls and a release calendar. They do not publish a Dutch employer's hours, a hire-quality result, or a works-council decision. A recruitment match score and a system that can observe staff are switches this role owns. The pages below do not show a customer count for either.

## S1. IBM removed the HRBP route for 21,000 managers, watched the score fall to −35, and says it would pilot next time

- Date: the page does not print one. The copyright line is 2026. The events it recounts run from about 2016 through 2025.
- Source: https://www.ibm.com/case-studies/ibm-transformation/ask-hr
- Who: Nickle LaMoreaux, senior vice president and chief human resources officer, IBM. IBM writing about IBM.
- What: By 2016 IBM had about 25 HR chatbots and folded them into one front door, AskHR. It then told all 21,000 first-line managers worldwide that dedicated HR business partner support would stop, and it shut the global HR email address and phone number. Managers could get support only through AskHR. Over the following year the internal Net Promoter Score fell to −35. IBM later put transactions behind the same door, including employee transfers and employment-verification letters. Between 2021 and 2024 AskHR, working with HR partners, was the single entry point for first-line managers and 85% of upline managers. IBM moved from NPS to CSAT and now reports +74. In 2025 it added multi-agent orchestration so AskHR could coordinate agents on a transaction. The lesson on the page, in LaMoreaux's words, is to find a small pain, automate that, make it good, then scale. IBM says that if it did this again it would pilot and iterate, and that culture overrides the technology. A footnote says the examples are illustrative, results vary, and statements about the future are goals.
- Number: NPS −35 after the cutover. CSAT +74 later. Nearly 90,000 employee transfers a year once transactions were added. In 2025, more than 16 million individual user messages, about 39,000 a day, about 51 interactions per IBMer per year. 21,000 first-line managers lost a dedicated HRBP route at the start. 85% of upline managers were on the single entry point by 2021–2024. No hours-saved figure, no HR headcount figure, and no error rate.
- Grade: E3 as the employer's own account of its operating-model change, including the failure. The footnote limits how far a reader should take the numbers.
- Flag: IBM sells the kind of assistant it describes. The 16 million messages are a 2025 volume, not a result from this quarter, and they are not the same figure as the 90–95% in S2. Do not import older IBM claims (budget cut, HRBP headcount, containment rate) from pages that were not opened.

## S7. Transparency, fines and a literacy duty already apply. Annex III duties for HR systems wait until 2 December 2027

- Date: the page dates itself July 2026 and does not print a day. It says Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 entered into force on 27 July 2026.
- Source: https://www.kinstellar.com/news-and-insights/detail/4619/the-ai-act-after-the-digital-omnibus-simplified-rules-delayed-deadlines-but-can-compliance-wait
- Who: Andrada Popescu, managing associate, Kinstellar. The firm has no Dutch office on this page. The Romania section is about Romanian authorities.
- What: The Digital Omnibus postponed Annex III high-risk duties from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027. Popescu's list for that annex includes recruitment, employee monitoring, performance evaluation, promotion, task allocation and termination. Product-embedded high-risk systems move to 2 August 2028. Not postponed: Article 50 transparency from 2 August 2026, so a person interacting with a system must be told, and generated or manipulated content must be marked. She names recruitment chatbots, candidate assistants, and generative tools in recruitment or employee communications. Enforcement applies from the same date, with fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Article 4 no longer requires a "sufficient" level of AI literacy. The employer must take measures so staff can develop it. People who oversee a high-risk system still need the competence for that oversight. Two further prohibited practices, generating non-consensual intimate imagery and generating child sexual abuse material, apply from 2 December 2026. Providers and deployers may process special-category data to test for bias, under a strict necessity test. Simplified documentation and a lower fine exposure, previously for SMEs, now also cover small mid-caps: fewer than 750 employees and turnover not above €150 million, or a balance sheet not above €129 million. Her close is that the extra months are for preparing the high-risk duties, not for pausing the duties already in force. She also says to check whether introducing a system triggers employee information and consultation.
- Number: the dates and the fine cap are the regulation as she states it. No enforcement count.
- Grade: E3 as a law-firm reading of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. The EUR-Lex text of the Omnibus was not opened. The AI Act text was not re-opened for this file.
- Flag: law-firm marketing, and the authority section is Romanian, not Dutch. A firm above the 750-employee line is outside the small-mid-cap relief she describes. The postponement is not a pause on telling a candidate they are talking to a bot.

## S8. A recruiter's final click does not take a scoring system off the high-risk list

- Date: the page does not print one. It summarises Commission draft guidelines of 19 May 2026, which is before this window. It says the draft is not binding and the final guidelines are not published.
- Source: https://www.praxikon.com/en/annex-iii/werkgelegenheid-personeelsbeheer
- Who: Praxikon, summarising the Commission draft. The draft itself and the EUR-Lex text linked from the page were not opened.
- What: Annex III point 4(a) is recruitment and selection. Point 4(b) is work terms, promotion, termination, task allocation from behaviour or traits, and performance monitoring. On Praxikon's reading of the draft, what matters is whether the output meaningfully influences an opportunity, an assessment or a working condition. A person making the final click does not change that. A system that assesses candidates or workers usually profiles them, and profiling blocks the Article 6(3) exception. Article 26(7): employers inform workers and their representatives before use. Praxikon's examples that can fall outside, if there is no assessment and no profiling: interview scheduling, turning a CV into fields without a score or a rank, a chatbot that only answers questions about the procedure, and anonymous productivity figures at team level. Examples it keeps inside: CV screening that ranks candidates, video-interview or personality analysis, targeted ads that use traits or behaviour to decide who sees a vacancy, rosters built from individual profiles, and scores used for promotion or reviews.
- Number: none.
- Grade: E3 as a published summary of a Commission draft. The draft is not a decision, and this page is not a supervisor.
- Flag: Praxikon sells classification and an evidence sprint. The examples can change when the guidance is final. A match score of the kind in S10 is the ranking example, not the "fields without a score" example.

## S9. In the Netherlands the product name does not decide the works-council right, and one decision can need both advice and consent

- Date: QUASA, 30 September 2026, 11:00. The SER page does not print a date.
- Source: https://quasa.io/nl/media/ai-controleert-werknemers-wanneer-de-ondernemingsraad-moet-instemmen
- Who: QUASA editorial desk. It cites Ondernemingsraad.nl, a Rijksoverheid page on works-council rights, and the SER. Those three pages were not opened. The SER page that was opened: https://www.ser.nl/nl/thema/medezeggenschap/belangrijke-themas/ai
- What: QUASA's route is to describe what the system does, not what it is called. A major technology or organisation change is an advice question under Article 25. A rule on working time, appointment, personnel data, assessment, or monitoring of presence, behaviour or performance is a consent question under Article 27. If a decision does both, ask for both. Capability counts: if managers can see individual activity or performance, it is not enough to say they will not use it yet. The council should be told which functions exist, who can see them, how long results are kept, and what they may be used for. Conditional examples on the page: a meeting transcript becomes a monitoring question if it is stored per person, searchable, or used in reviews; a roster that changes working-time rules needs consent, and may also need advice; individual productivity scores are monitoring, and if they feed reviews, promotion or pay, those policies too; recruitment ranking is an appointment and selection rule; a generative assistant is not monitoring until individual usage stats are used to follow or assess staff. The SER page says to discuss coming AI policy in an Article 24 meeting, that one proposed decision can have both an advice part and a consent part, and that the council can train and hire an expert. If there is no agreement, the Bedrijfscommissie is the route it names, and Articles 25 and 27 have their own objection routes. The SER's four risk examples still list a chatbot as minimal risk, and the page does not mention the 2 December 2027 postponement in S7.
- Number: none.
- Grade: E3 for the SER as the public body that publishes the works-council guidance, with the staleness flag. E3 for QUASA as an explainer that cites that page and two official pages. Not a court decision. The underlying statute text was not opened for this file.
- Flag: the SER page has no date and its chatbot example is behind Article 50, which S7 says has applied since 2 August 2026. QUASA is an editorial desk, not the council or a court. Consent refused is a subdistrict-court question under Article 27(4). This page does not walk that step.

## What this role should not treat as settled

No opened page shows a Dutch employer with hours returned, a hire still in seat at 90 days, or a works-council vote on an HR agent. IBM's −35 and +74 are IBM's own scores, years apart, with a footnote that results vary. The 90–95% is one executive's remark and is not the 16 million messages. Workday's 50-to-42 is a demo. "Weeks to days" and "25 open jobs" are slogans. Bersin's doubling of revenue per employee is a belief on a partnership page. The bias tests Godson mentions are not on the IT Brief page.

The duties that already apply are the transparency notice, the fine framework, the literacy measures, and the Dutch advice and consent questions. The heavy Annex III duties for recruitment and worker management are dated 2 December 2027 on the Kinstellar page, and a final click does not take a scoring tool off that list. The SER page does not say this, and it still calls a chatbot minimal risk.
