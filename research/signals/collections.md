## S3. Collection files fail on the first letter, and a court can refuse the costs on its own

- Date: 5 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.creditexpo.nl/juridische-scherpte-begint-bij-de-eerste-aanmaning/
- Who: Credit Expo. The source line says "Bron: LegalMike." LegalMike sells legal AI to collection firms, bailiffs, and legal teams. No firm publishes an error rate on the page.
- What: Since the Collection Services Quality Act (Wet kwaliteit incassodienstverlening, Wki), the Justice and Security Inspectorate supervises the market. The page says file errors and misleading debtor communication lead to reports and enforcement. Courts test consumer claims of their own motion, and can refuse the collection costs or the whole claim. The failures named: a 14-day letter that does not meet the statute or was sent to the wrong address, statutory interest or collection costs calculated wrong, and general terms that were never handed over or that contain a term a court treats as unfair. The page says these mistakes are made early, often by staff who are not lawyers, and show up only when the case goes to court. LegalMike maps the legal points in a file. Gerechtsdeurwaarderskantoor Flanderijn is listed to talk about daily use at Credit Expo on 5 November 2026, 14:45 to 15:15, in Dexter 19. That talk has not happened.
- Number: no error rate, no fine, no count of refused claims.
- Grade: E3 as a vendor event page describing legal failure modes.
- Flag: the page is about consumer claims. A business-to-business logistics book is a different debtor, and this page does not say the same tests apply. Do not treat a future seminar as a result. LegalMike is selling the check it describes.

## S4. From 1 October, a news report says an unregistered collector can lose the costs and the interest

- Date: 14 October 2026.
- Source: https://vastgoedinsider.nl/let-op-je-vastgoedbeheerder-mag-niet-zomaar-huurachterstanden-innen/
- Who: Vastgoed Insider, byline Redactie. The claims are attributed to Wouter Veldink, director of Level Real Estate, via a LinkedIn post. That post was not opened. The statute text was not opened.
- What: The article says article 18 of the Wki has been in force since 1 October 2026. Veldink's reading, as reported: a debtor need not pay an unregistered collector, no costs are owed for that party's work, the defect can follow the file to a lawyer, and interest can be affected from the first written payment request. If costs or interest are claimed in court, names and registration numbers must be stated, or the court may treat the party as unregistered. Collecting your own invoice is not the Wki problem he describes. The problem is outsourcing the chase. Level Real Estate says it reviewed its own process. The article is about rent arrears. Part of the page is a membership offer at €19.50 a month or €195 a year.
- Number: 1 October 2026 as the in-force date the article states. No count of files or of lost interest.
- Grade: E3 as a news report of one director's reading. Not the text of the law.
- Flag: rent, not a trade receivable. Do not treat the LinkedIn paraphrase as the statute. Do not treat a landlord's property manager as this team's collection desk. The registration question still matters if a third party sends the reminder.
