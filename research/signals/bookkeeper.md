## S1. An AFAS partner says a one-to-one match is booked and approved for payment

- Date: 14 October 2026.
- Source: https://irixs.nl/kennisbank/afas-profit-8
- Who: Irixs, an AFAS partner. The finance section has no author byline and no customer count. A LinkedIn video linked from the page was not opened.
- What: The heading is "AFAS Profit 8 maakt een einde aan het handmatig controleren van inkoopfacturen." The text says Profit reads UBL and PDF invoices automatically down to the line. If the invoice matches one-to-one with the purchase order or the goods receipt, Profit books the invoice and approves it for payment (boekt en fiateert). The same paragraph says a built-in Peppol connection for sending and receiving is free (gratis). Elsewhere on the page, DKIM for mail sent from AFAS is described as mandatory in Profit 8, and the advice is to arrange it "vóór 13 juli." The page is dated 14 October.
- Number: none. No invoice count, no price list, no share of invoices that match one-to-one.
- Grade: E2. A partner describing a product.
- Flag: "maakt een einde aan" is a slogan. "Gratis" is the partner's word. This note did not open a price page. The July DKIM date is still on an October page, so the page is carrying a deadline that has passed. Do not treat a one-to-one match rule as a claim that every supplier invoice will post itself. A PDF that does not match still needs a person, and the page does not say otherwise.

## S4. The buyer-side clock, on a Peppol seller's reading, is five days after the invoice arrives

- Date: 25 September 2026.
- Source: https://www.peppol.nu/blog-items/vida-nederland/
- Who: Justin De Jager, peppol.nu. He is reading the cabinet letter of 11 September 2026 and the directive. EUR-Lex and the letter on open.overheid.nl were not opened. The accountant note uses the same page for the sales invoice. This item is the purchase side.
- What: He writes that the Netherlands is not using the option to skip buyer reporting. From 1 July 2030, on his reading, a Dutch buyer reports each intra-EU acquisition and each purchase where VAT is reverse-charged, at the latest five days after the invoice is received. He attributes that five days to article 262. He writes that the cabinet expects bookkeeping packages to do this semi-automatically, and that this is only light work if purchase invoices arrive as structured files and are booked quickly. An invoice that sits for two weeks in a mailbox or an approval round misses the deadline. The sales-side 10 days and the statement that a PDF is not an e-invoice are on the same page.
- Number: 5 days after receipt, as he states the rule, from 1 July 2030. The bill is not law on this page.
- Grade: E3 as a vendor explainer. Not the statute.
- Flag: the site sells Peppol. Do not treat the five days as enacted Dutch law. A logistics company that buys across the EU is the reader he is addressing. Domestic purchase invoices are a different sentence on the page, and he says the bill still has to specify the domestic timetable.
