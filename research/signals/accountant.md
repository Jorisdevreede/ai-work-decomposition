## S1. The study that says "junior accountants" tested licensed CPAs, and left the rest of the job out

- Date: 1 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.mercor.com/blog/human-baselines-for-benchmarks-ai-now-outperforms-junior-accountants/
- Who: Aden Barton, Researcher at Mercor. The full paper PDF was not opened. The score chart is in the controller note. This item is who sat the test and what it measured.
- What: The title is "AI Now Outperforms Junior Accountants." The opening says "we hired 12 junior accountants." The method section says participants averaged about five and a half years of accounting experience and all were licensed CPAs. They completed four month-end scenarios. The authors expected about 30% from a junior and 55% from a mid-level accountant. Barton writes that the tasks test detail, searching files, and following instructions, which is what the models are best at. He writes that the job they did not measure is client communication, asking the right questions, and tacit context. A task author is quoted: "the traps [hidden in the task] are realistic, and the conditions and the scoring are what drove the results." Because models scored perfectly, the augmentation comparison the study was designed for could not be run.
- Number: 12 people. About 5.5 years. Expected scores 30% junior and 55% mid-level. Opus 5 at $0.21 per criterion met against $10.35 for an accountant at the US median wage, which the page calls 49 times more.
- Grade: E3 as the vendor's own write-up of its study.
- Flag: Mercor sells the benchmark. Do not quote the title and skip the method section. Do not treat a US median wage as a Dutch salary. The perfect score is the four-scenario study, not a statutory close.

## S4. A Peppol seller: a PDF is not an e-invoice, and the 10-day domestic deadline is his reading

- Date: 25 September 2026.
- Source: https://www.peppol.nu/blog-items/vida-nederland/
- Who: Justin De Jager, on peppol.nu, a Peppol seller's site. He is reading the cabinet letter of 11 September 2026 and Directive (EU) 2025/516. The EUR-Lex page and the letter on open.overheid.nl were not opened. The finance-director note already records that the network is not chosen. This item is the invoice an accountant issues.
- What: He writes that article 217 defines an e-invoice as a structured file that can be processed automatically, and that a PDF is not one. Article 222, as he states the directive, requires the invoice within 10 days of the chargeable event for intra-EU supplies and for supplies where VAT is reverse-charged, and within 10 days of an advance payment. A summary invoice for a calendar month is due within 10 days of month-end. For domestic invoices he writes that the cabinet letter says the national system "closely follows" the EU deadlines, and that the bill still has to specify whether the 10 days apply at home. His planning advice is to assume 10 days. He also writes that the zero rate on an intra-EU supply fails if the supplier misses the reporting duty or files wrong data, unless the supplier can properly explain the failure. He cites article 138(1a) for that link.
- Number: 10 days, as the directive for the intra-EU case and as his reading for the domestic case. The domestic e-invoicing date he reports from the letter is 1 July 2030. The bill is not law on this page.
- Grade: E3 as a secondary explainer of a directive and a cabinet letter. The primary texts were not opened.
- Flag: the site sells Peppol connections. Do not treat his domestic 10-day advice as the text of a Dutch statute. Do not treat a PDF by email as an e-invoice under the definition he quotes. A separate bill on a single VAT registration is mentioned on the page with a Senate date of 6 October 2026. No later parliamentary record was opened, so that line is not used here.

## S5. The same page: the small-business scheme is out of the domestic duty, and cash-basis firms are not

- Date: 25 September 2026.
- Source: https://www.peppol.nu/blog-items/vida-nederland/
- Who: Justin De Jager, as in S4. EY's advice is mentioned. EY's own page was not opened.
- What: He writes that firms on the small-business scheme (KOR) get no domestic e-invoicing duty. They must still be able to receive e-invoices, and intra-EU acquisitions can still bring a reporting duty. If they agree commercially to send e-invoices, he says they need not report those invoice data. Firms on the cash basis, his examples are shops, hairdressers, and hospitality, are not exempt, even though he says EY advised that they should be. Their business-to-business invoices fall under the duty from 1 July 2030, on his reading of the letter. The finance-director note records the cabinet's KOR threshold of €20,000 from the government page. This page says the KOR is the exemption the cabinet is relying on, and it does not restate the euro cap.
- Number: 1 July 2030, as the date he attributes to the cabinet letter. No count of firms.
- Grade: E3 as a vendor reading of the cabinet's stated intention. Not statute.
- Flag: a logistics company that invoices other businesses is outside the KOR story on this page. Do not treat the cash-basis exception as something EY's page confirmed. That page was not opened.
