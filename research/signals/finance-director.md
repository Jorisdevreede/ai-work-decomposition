# Signals: Finance director

Window: 16 July 2026 to 14 October 2026. Written 14 October 2026 by SCOUT.
Each link below was opened in this session. Grades: E2 a named product does this today; E3 a documented case, a survey page, or a legal source you can read. Nothing here is E4. Vendor and marketing pieces are marked.

What shows up for this role: spend, governance, and which finance work still needs a named person. The freshest numbers are a planning vendor's survey of finance executives in four countries. They report overspend, high confidence in the return, and reporting work that does not shrink. A product release this week puts accounting autopilot on a 2027 calendar and leaves review with the accounting team. Dutch e-invoicing is a multi-year capacity and data-protection decision. The bill is not law, and the exchange network is not chosen.

## S1. A CFO survey says 83% overspent on usage-priced AI, and confidence in the return stayed high

- Date: the page schema prints `datePublished` 7 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.pigment.com/reports/cfo-index/q3-2026
- Who: Pigment, a planning-software vendor. The page describes 2,000 CFOs and finance executives. The schema names the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and Germany. The front matter also says "globally." No Netherlands breakout. Susan Phan, Pigment's incoming CFO, is named only as the host of a webinar on the findings.
- What: 83% say consumption-based costs pushed AI spending above expectations. The median overrun sits in the 11–25% band. The chart shows 13.0% did not exceed the budget, 18.1% were 1–10% over, 26.7% were 11–25% over, 21.8% were 25–50% over, 12.3% were 51–75% over, 3.4% were 75–100% over, 0.9% were more than 100% over, and 3.8% cannot measure. The prose rounds "cannot measure" to 4% and says 38% are more than 25% over and 17% are more than 50% over, which brings the average to about 27%. The expected rise in next year's AI budget fell from 19.1% to 15.6%, which the page says is lower than this year's average overspend. Confidence that AI is delivering a return averages 8.2 out of 10. The chart puts 49.7% at 9 or 10 and 12.2% at 6 or below. The prose rounds those to 50% and 12%. On the same page, "unclear ROI" as a barrier rose 3.2 points to 27.6%.
- Number: 83% over budget. Average overrun about 27%. Next-year budget rise expected at 15.6%, down from 19.1%. ROI confidence 8.2/10. No response rate and no fieldwork date on the page.
- Grade: E3 as a published vendor survey. Self-reported. Not a set of invoices.
- Flag: Pigment sells planning software and links an "AI Investment Planner" under the spend section. The percentages can overlap with other questions on the same page. There is no Dutch firm in the write-up. Do not treat 8.2/10 as a measured return.

## S6. A CFO advisor: routine reconciliation can run, tax estimates and cash allocation stay with a person

- Date: 25 September 2026.
- Source: https://www.techtarget.com/enterprise-software/tip/What-CFOs-need-to-know-about-AI-agent-governance-in-accounting
- Who: James Kofalt, president of DX4 Research, writing a tip for TechTarget. He notes 16 years at SAP and a product-manager role at Microsoft Business Solutions. No company is the subject.
- What: Start with an inventory of tools, purpose, and guardrails, plus a data glossary, so decisions are not a black box. Routine account reconciliation and accounts-payable automation can run with people on the exceptions. Strategic purchases, tax estimates, and cash allocation need mandatory human review and approval. Require timestamped logs of the decision, the data lineage, and the reasoning, ideally inside the finance systems. He names SOX, the US Sarbanes-Oxley Act, as a reason the trail matters. Assign an AI governance lead inside finance, working with legal and compliance. Track productivity, error or incident rates, and compliance, and run periodic model audits and bias checks. An EY page on AI investment is linked and was not opened.
- Number: none.
- Grade: E3 as published advice from a named practitioner. Not a case.
- Flag: advice. SOX is a US control. A Dutch close still needs a person who will sign, which is the point he is making in other words. No return is measured.

## S7. Shadow AI: the policy does not cover the paste, the update, or the workpaper the auditor cannot rebuild

- Date: 5 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.techtarget.com/it-strategy/tip/Potential-risks-from-shadow-AI-use-in-accounting
- Who: Kevin Beaver, Principle Logic, LLC, an information-security consultant, writing a tip for TechTarget. No company count.
- What: Four risks. Payroll registers and client tax files pasted into a personal chatbot become subject to that provider's terms, which may include training. Ask people which tools they use, what they paste, and which problem each tool solves, then give an approved tool. Blocking tools without an approved alternative drives secret use. An auditor who asks how an estimate was built may find that the prompt and the output sit in a personal account, that the source was not kept, or that the model added an assumption because the answer sounded right. That work is also outside the company's collection tools, retention schedule, and legal hold. AI features inside the ERP, expense tools, email, spreadsheets, browser extensions, and meeting transcription can switch on in an update. Treat AI as another system under the access, inventory, retention, and vendor review the department already runs.
- Number: none.
- Grade: E3 as published advice. Not an incident report.
- Flag: the "sounded right" failure is described, not counted. A LinkedIn post is linked from the offboarding bullet and was not opened.

## What this role should not treat as settled

No finance team here publishes days to close, a forecast the business trusts, or an AI budget against invoices. Pigment's bars are one vendor survey, self-reported, with no Dutch slice and with questions that can overlap. The 8.2/10 return score sits on the same page as a rising "unclear ROI" barrier. The reporting-hours paradox is the page's reading of its maturity split, and that split is not defined in the text. Workday's accounting autopilot is a plan for early adopters in early 2027, and the release says unreleased features may not ship. Kofalt and Beaver describe controls and failure modes without a count. The e-invoicing dates are the cabinet's intention. The saving per invoice is asserted and not numbered. The network is not chosen on the pages that were opened.
