# Signals: Learning and Development

Window: 16 July 2026 to 14 October 2026. Written 14 October 2026 by SCOUT.
Each link below was opened in this session. Grades: E2 a named product does this today; E3 a documented case or a legal source with a page you can read. Nothing here is E4. Vendor and marketing pieces are marked.

What shows up for this role: L&D teams say they use AI, mostly to make content faster, and a minority put it in front of learners as practice or coaching. The measured internal case is about the quality of manager feedback, not course completion. The legal duty on the employer is to take measures that support AI literacy. It does not set a level, a course or a certificate. A Dutch industry course on sale this month still describes the old duty and hands out a certificate.

## S1. Taylor and Vinauskaitė: most L&D teams use AI, and content is still the core

- Date: 7 October 2026. Learning News.
- Source: https://learningnews.com/news/learning-news/2026/ai-use-grows-but-lds-core-work-holds-steady
- Who: Donald H. Taylor and Eglė Vinauskaitė. The article reports their fourth annual AI-in-L&D study, "Around the Core". The report file itself was not opened. The article names case sketches at Randstad Enterprise, KPMG UK, EY and International Aviation Technical College Riyadh, without numbers for any of them.
- What: Use has spread from drafting content into analysis, design decisions and departmental admin. Evaluation and analytics moved from eighth to third of ten use categories between 2023 and 2026. A "break-out" subset says the rest of the organisation now asks them for different things, and those teams more often put AI in front of learners for practice, coaching and feedback. The authors' own line is that the next phase may be about L&D's role, not about the tool. They also say the break-out finding is an early signal, not proof that AI caused a change in role. Respondents were self-selecting, with little overlap between years.
- Number: 531 practitioners in 55 countries. More than two thirds say their organisation uses AI in L&D, against about 40% two years ago and more than half last year. Of expected benefits, 44.5% picked data analysis to support decisions, up from 31.9% in 2024. Faster content creation stayed the leading expected benefit. Of 298 workplace L&D and HR respondents, 43 (about 14%) said the organisation now approaches them for different things. In that subset, 38% described AI in front of learners, against 17% of other workplace teams, and 45% described operational use, against 17%. Only 37% of the break-out group ranked faster content among their top three expected benefits, against 63% of the others. Barriers: readiness (skills, where to start, business case) 36%, down from 49% in 2024. Integration, cost, time or resistance 66%. Data privacy and security 52%, the most common single barrier.
- Grade: E3 as a published write-up of a named survey. Not a representative sample.
- Flag: The report is sponsored by Sana, Docebo, OpenSesame and Speexx. Learning News states the self-selection limit. Do not treat "more than two thirds" as the profession.

## S2. BambooHR: of the time staff spend with AI, more goes to fixing it than to the work

- Date: 1 September 2026. Fieldwork 26 June to 15 July 2026, so the answers were collected up to the day before this window. The release is inside it.
- Source: https://www.bamboohr.com/about-bamboohr/press-release/bamboohr-research-redesigning-work-ai-performance-review
- Who: Nicole Csizar, senior director of HR services, BambooHR. Survey prepared by Method Research and fielded by RepData.
- What: Csizar's point for a learning team is the gap between an instant answer and the judgment to know what to do with it. She says efficiency should not replace mentorship. The release also says 35% of workers say knowledge transfer in their organisation now runs mainly through AI tools rather than people, and 54% of organisations do not have a clear, documented AI usage policy that is consistently communicated. Gen Z (54%) and Millennials (43%) are more likely than Gen X (39%) and Baby Boomers (21%) to say junior staff rely on AI in ways that limit growth. HR has updated 43% of job descriptions on average to reflect AI, while 75% of workers say AI has already changed how they do the job. The full data story linked from the release was not opened.
- Number: 1,608 full-time salaried US desk workers, including 520 HR professionals at manager level or above. 87 minutes a day with AI, which BambooHR scales to about 47 eight-hour days a year (they print 22,526 minutes). 42% of that AI time is troubleshooting and prompt iteration. 35% is productive work. They call the gap about 20 days a year. 42% plus 35% is not 100%. The rest of the day is not described. 65% feel confident and enthusiastic. 58% say time savings is the main motive. VPs and the C-suite spend 101 minutes a day, individual contributors 54. 63% of organisations have raised AI tool budgets. 59% use a personal AI account for work. Of that group, 71% have put client data, strategy or other sensitive company information into a tool the employer cannot see.
- Grade: E3. Named firm, named field house, dates and a method paragraph. US desk workers. Vendor-commissioned.
- Flag: BambooHR sells the platform. The 20 days is their scaling of self-reported minutes, not a time-and-motion study.

## S3. Workday: a check-in agent moved the share of feedback rated "Good"

- Date: indexed 9 October 2026. The article does not print a date.
- Source: https://blog.workday.com/en-us/how-workdays-hr-team-is-reimagining-workflows-with-sana.html
- Who: Workday's own talent team. Named user: Jim Delia, SVP of global revenue operations.
- What: The agent drafts manager check-ins from connections the employee has approved (mail, Slack, documents, meetings, Salesforce, GitHub, Jira, Workday goals) and pushes past generic praise. The rubric is Situation-Behavior-Impact. This is the learning-relevant slice of a longer internal story. The same page's calibration agent is an HRBP tool, not an L&D course.
- Number: Of nearly 12,200 manager write-ups, 36% were rated "Good" before the push. High-quality feedback rose 50% in Q1 and 44% in Q2. Among agent users the share rated "Good" rose 16 percentage points, against 7 points for people who did not use the agent. Delia says prep fell by over 80%: a quarterly business review became responses in 20 minutes, against nearly two hours. Workday says the hard part was that baselines were not set before the agent existed.
- Grade: E3, the vendor measuring its own managers. The non-user gap is the part that is harder to dismiss as a slogan.
- Flag: Workday sells Sana. No outside auditor.

## S5. MHR: the learning team uses an agent to draft and check reading level, and blocks time in the calendar

- Date: 9 October 2026. HRD Connect, in partnership with MHR.
- Source: https://www.hrdconnect.com/2026/10/09/ai-is-changing-what-we-learn-is-lnd-changing-fast-enough/
- Who: Chloe Bryars, head of learning and enablement, MHR. Interview at MHR's World of Work 2026.
- What: Staff get education on cybersecurity, data and safe AI use, including how to work with agents. Bryars says the skill past prompting is critical thinking: challenge the first answer. Her own team uses an AI agent to draft learning content and check reading level and consistency, with a person still reviewing. They are blocking development time in consultants' calendars rather than hoping for a gap between customers, and they ask afterwards whether the time was used and what got in the way. She says uptake rose and blockers fell, and gives no count. Measurement is set with the manager before the course is built, then collected just after, at about six weeks, and at about six months. AI can cluster the comments. She calls it a support for reading the comments, not the decision. She also worries that if junior tasks disappear, the next cohort has nowhere to learn the job. Skills mapping in professional services is in progress, not finished.
- Number: MHR research, cited but not linked as a separate report, says 94% of leaders believe L&D improves performance. No sample size on this page. No hours saved. No completion rate.
- Grade: E3 for a named practitioner describing her own team's practice. The 94% is an unnamed internal or company study.
- Flag: The page is labelled in partnership with MHR. The uptake line is her judgment.

## S6. European Commission: Article 4 is a duty to support literacy, and no "sufficient" level is required

- Date: page last updated 13 October 2026.
- Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy
- Who: European Commission, DG Connect policy page.
- What: Article 4 has applied since 2 February 2025. The Digital Omnibus on AI amended it. The page says that amendment entered into force in mid-July 2026. It does not print the day. Providers and deployers take measures to support AI literacy of staff and of other people who operate or use the systems on their behalf, taking account of knowledge, experience, education, context, and the people the system is used on. No specific level, and no "sufficient" level, is mandated. The Commission and Member States support that effort, especially for smaller firms, including by publishing examples. National market-surveillance authorities supervise Article 4 from 2 August 2026. A living repository holds more than 40 practices. Copying one does not create a presumption of compliance. The surveys that filled the repository ran in late 2024 and in spring 2025, outside this window.
- Number: "more than 40" practices. Not a result.
- Grade: E3. The Commission's own page.
- Flag: A teaser on the same page for the AI-literacy Q&A still says Article 4 requires providers and deployers to "ensure a sufficient level". That is the wording the body of this page says was dropped. The Q&A page itself was not opened. Do not treat the teaser as the current rule.

## S7. Praxikon: no course, no hours, no certificate, and human oversight of high-risk systems is a later and stricter duty

- Date: obligations checked against the source on 1 October 2026. Legally reviewed 30 July 2026. Zahed Ashkara.
- Source: https://www.praxikon.com/en/ai-act/artikel/4
- Who: Praxikon. Ashkara is an AI and privacy lawyer. The page sells a case check and his time.
- What: He dates the amended wording from 27 July 2026, which fits the Commission's "mid-July" and is more precise. The reproduced duty matches the Commission page: take measures that support literacy. A certificate, a minimum number of hours and a test are not prescribed. Training can be one measure, next to instructions and practice. He says the Commission has said no certificate is needed. That Q&A was not opened here, so the "no certificate" line is his attribution. For high-risk systems, Article 26(2) still requires the deployer to put human oversight in the hands of people with the competence, training and authority. He dates that Annex III duty from 2 December 2027, and Annex I from 2 August 2028. Article 4 itself has no separate fixed fine. From August 2026, national authorities can use proportionate measures under national law. He says the Commission has also said that staff using ChatGPT or Copilot for work should be told about specific risks such as hallucinations. Again, that is his reading of the Q&A.
- Number: the dates above.
- Grade: E3 as a lawyer's page that quotes the amended article and links EUR-Lex. The Official Journal did not render in this session.
- Flag: He sells the advice. The useful distinction for an L&D lead is Article 4 (measures, now, no set level) against Article 26(2) (competence of the person who oversees a high-risk system, later).

## S8. FME College: a three-hour Dutch course still says the Act requires "sufficient" knowledge, and it ends with a certificate

- Date: page opened 14 October 2026. The event is Thursday 29 October, 13:00–16:00, Postillion Bunnik. The year is not printed on the event line. 29 October 2026 is a Thursday.
- Source: https://www.fme.nl/agenda/ai-geletterdheid-de-praktijk
- Who: FME College, members only. Trainer: MatchPerfect. Contact: Petra van Buren, account manager.
- What: Three hours for staff who use ChatGPT, Copilot or similar tools. No technical background required. Topics include what AI is, prompting, checking output, privacy, security, bias and human oversight, plus a block on the AI Act. The page says the Act requires organisations to give staff sufficient knowledge and skills for responsible use. That is the pre-amendment reading. S6 and S7, both updated in October, say the opposite: no sufficient level is mandated. The course ends with a quiz and a participation certificate offered as proof of knowledge. Bring a laptop and, preferably, an account on one of the tools.
- Number: €295 per person, excluding VAT. Three hours. No completion or behaviour result, because the session has not been run yet on this page.
- Grade: E2 for the offer: a named Dutch industry body is selling this on this date. E0 if anyone treats the certificate as compliance.
- Flag: The legal sentence on the page is behind the text the Commission published. A certificate is a record an employer may choose to keep. It is not what Article 4 asks for.

## What this role should not treat as settled

No opened page shows a Dutch employer cutting time-to-skill, raising completion, or proving that a course changed the work. Microsoft describes the learning agent and withholds the number. MHR says uptake rose and does not say by how much. The Taylor figures are a self-selected, vendor-sponsored survey, and the authors say so. BambooHR's 20 days is a vendor's scaling of what US desk workers remember, from fieldwork that closed on 15 July. The literacy duty is real, it is in force, and it is a duty to take fitting measures and be able to show them. It is not a three-hour certificate.
