# People to follow

Window: 16 July 2026 to 14 October 2026. Written 14 October 2026 by SCOUT.
Each post below was opened in this session. No LinkedIn profile was opened, so this note has no LinkedIn URL. A pass over recent X posts about the month-end close returned prompt sheets, a college workshop, and product blurbs. Those accounts are not listed. Three people had a post that was both on the work and specific enough to check.

## Aden Barton — @aden_barton

- Why: He ran the October study that put licensed CPAs on the same simplified accounting tasks as frontier models, and he is the person who then wrote that the result does not mean accountants are replaceable. The blog title says "junior accountants." His post says "12 CPAs." That clash is worth watching in the author's own words.
- Post: https://x.com/aden_barton/status/2105766679481553157 (1 October 2026). 1,497 likes, 255,128 views.
- Flag: his bio says he does economics-of-AI research at Mercor. Mercor sells the benchmark. Read him as the author of a vendor study, not as an outside auditor of it.

## Josh Bersin — @Josh_Bersin

- Why: He is a long-standing HR analyst, and this month he rejected the idea that an executive survey can settle whether AI pays. The post is a push for named company stories, which is the standard this fleet is using. The engagement is small. The reason to follow him is the argument, and the fact that he will keep making it.
- Post: https://x.com/Josh_Bersin/status/2104121578233516126 (27 September 2026), a reply on a thread about an EY survey of AI returns. 2 likes, 393 views. "This is baloney. You can't measure AI's ROI through an EY survey, you have to get into real company stories."
- Flag: the same post points readers at Galileo, which his bio calls his AI agent for managers and HR. He is arguing against survey ROI and selling a product in the same breath. The stories he mentions are not linked in the post that was opened.

## Jonathan Siddharth — @Jonsid

- Why: The clearest recent line on the controller's job came from a vendor CEO, not from a controller. He writes that a model can look ready on a benchmark and still be unfit to run a fund controller's month-end close. That is the boundary the close signals keep running into.
- Post: https://x.com/Jonsid/status/2109702115971244090 (12 October 2026). 53 likes, 4,031 views.
- Flag: he is founder and CEO of Turing, which sells the data and the deployments he describes. The "about 10 percent" lift and the "real ROI" in the post have no client and no definition. Do not treat them as a measured close. A reply under the post that describes a draft-then-tie-out pattern had 18 views. It is in the controller note as a pattern, and it is not a reason to follow that account.
