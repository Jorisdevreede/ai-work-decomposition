# Signals: Compensation and benefits

Window: 16 July 2026 to 14 October 2026. Written 14 October 2026 by SCOUT.
Each link below was opened in this session. Grades: E2 a named product does this today; E3 a documented case or a legal source with a page you can read. Nothing here is E4. Vendor and marketing pieces are marked.

What shows up for this role: the live work is pricing jobs whose content moved faster than the band, and deciding whether AI use itself earns more pay. The tools on sale this quarter draft market findings and store job categories. None of the opened pages shows a Dutch employer running an AI merit cycle, or a measured closure of a pay gap. ## S1. The Dutch pay-transparency bill is still not in force, and the works council gets a vote on the job-evaluation system

- Date: the page does not print a publication date. The debates it names, 7 December 2026 and 11 January 2027, place the text inside this window.
- Source: https://www.randstad.nl/werkgevers/hr-wet-en-regelgeving/actuele-wetgeving/wet-loontransparantie
- Who: Randstad Netherlands, writing for employers. The ministry handreiking linked from the page is dated 15 April 2026 on the government URL and was not opened.
- What: Directive (EU) 2023/970. The national deadline of 7 June 2026 was missed. The bill went to the Tweede Kamer on 21 May 2026. After the two debates the Tweede Kamer votes, then the Eerste Kamer. The target of 1 January 2027 is no longer achievable. A new start date is not set. It will be fixed later by royal decree, and parts of the law can start on different days. For a compensation team the operative duties, once the law starts, are a job-evaluation and classification system on four factors (skills, responsibilities, effort, working conditions), salary or a band before pay negotiation (not necessarily in the vacancy), no asking for salary history, and, within two months of a request, the person's own pay plus the average of colleagues doing equal or equivalent work, split by sex, not a named colleague. Under 50 staff there is no duty to give access to the criteria for pay progression. The criteria for setting pay, and the pay levels, still have to be accessible. Reporting starts at 100 staff. In the current draft, 150 or more report on 2027 by 7 June 2028 (250 or more every year, 150 to 249 every three years). Employers with 100 to 149 report on 2030 by 7 June 2031. The government will fix the exact dates later, and delay can still move them. Agency workers count in FTE toward the 100. The lender supplies the pay data. The works council has a consent right on the evaluation system, the criteria, the grouping of people who do equal or equivalent work, and the approach to unexplained gaps. It has no consent right on the report itself. The employer asks the council whether the figures are right. A gap of 5% or more that is still unexplained after six months triggers a joint pay evaluation with the council. Any unexplained gap has to be fixed, including one under 5%. A pay-secrecy clause that stops an employee checking equal pay is void. Interns on a stageovereenkomst do not count. A bbl trainee on an employment contract does.
- Number: the inspectorate can order compliance. The current bill caps an administrative fine at €11,000. If the transparency rules were broken, the employer has to prove the gap is not discrimination, unless it was a small and clearly unintentional error.
- Grade: E3 as a staffing firm's reading of a bill that is not in force.
- Flag: Randstad places agency workers. This is not a measured pay gap at a named employer.

## S6. Zapier is designing a pilot to pay marketers for pipeline they bring in with AI. It has not run

- Date: 8 October 2026. TechTarget.
- Source: https://www.techtarget.com/it-strategy/news/366651805/Zapier-explores-extra-pay-for-employees-driving-AI-gains
- Who: Tim Murphy, TechTarget. Dan Slagen, Zapier's chief AI transformation officer and chief marketing officer, speaking at GAI World 2026. Also quoted: Manish Jain, founder of Strategic Horizon Research, and Jeremy Minich, a senior solutions engineering manager at Freshworks.
- What: One employee automated work that would have filled two open requisitions, gave the requisitions back, and asked for a share of the savings. Marketers have asked to be paid a percentage of the pipeline they create with AI. Zapier plans a three- to six-month pilot that pays selected marketers on the qualified pipeline they bring in. Credit is the unsolved part: a new name is easier to attribute than a contact already in the database, or a prospect sales also worked. Slagen says finance has kept an open mind and that the conversations have been difficult. His line on the difference: he would pay a vendor $100,000 to generate $10 million of pipeline, and "the second you add in, 'Oh, it's a person asking for it,' all the alarm bells start to go off and finance freaks out." Jain's view, from a separate interview, is that employees should not be paid for using AI. They should be recognised for value that is measurable, strategic and reusable, and for the work of documenting it so the company is not stuck paying the only person who understands it. Minich says Freshworks staff worry that disclosing an efficiency gain could threaten the job.
- Number: two requisitions handed back. A $100,000 / $10 million example Slagen used for a vendor, not a Zapier result. No pilot outcome.
- Grade: E3 for a named executive describing a plan. E0 if the pilot is treated as done.
- Flag: Conference remarks. No written plan, no cohort, no baseline.

## S7. Payscale: employers are rewriting jobs for AI and have not agreed how to pay for it

- Date: the preview page does not print a dateline. Fieldwork was August 2026. CFO.com of 24 September 2026 already quotes the same survey, so the preview existed by then. The page says the full report is released in November 2026.
- Source: https://www.payscale.com/featured-content/ai-workforce-impact-preview
- Who: Payscale. Surveys fielded online by Dynata. Employer respondents are HR, compensation and talent-acquisition people at organisations with 100 or more employees.
- What: The page's own warning is that the answers are stated intent, not completed action, and that they may not generalise below 100 employees. The approaches do not form a clean split. Paying a premium, treating AI as a baseline, freezing the structure, and still deciding are listed together and overlap. One worked example on the page: for a Business Process Analyst II, agentic AI and machine learning might be worth 9% higher median pay, while the same skills on a Machine Learning Engineer might be worth 0% because they are already assumed. Lightcast postings, as Payscale reads them, grew 8.7 times in the AI sector since 2022. Real estate saw the largest rise in posted wages for those jobs, at 158%. The top occupational groups for AI-skill postings include marketing managers, not only engineering.
- Number: employer survey n=500. Employee survey n=1,000. 61% of employers are rewriting job descriptions because of AI. 49% say salary structures have not kept pace. 48% say current benchmarking no longer reflects the AI skills the job needs. 40% cannot find people with enough AI fluency. 74% plan to invest in upskilling in the next 12 months. 56% of employees say they should be paid more for developing AI skills. 58% are paying a premium now or in the next 12 months. 23% paid a premium at first and now treat AI fluency as required. 19% are keeping the existing structure. 13% are still deciding. Those four do not add to 100%.
- Grade: E3. Named field house, month, sample size and a limitations paragraph. Opt-in. Not a probability sample.
- Flag: Payscale sells benchmarking software (Ascent, JobNav, Paycycle) on the same page. The 9% is an illustration, not a market rule.

Do not merge that 55% with the August preview in S7. S7's 61% is a later survey and does not print the 55%.
- What: The piece argues that a role can change inside a quarter while the band still describes last year's job. Ruffini says the engineering work on his team moved from writing code to directing tools and catching what they get wrong, and that his team now reprices when the work changes rather than waiting for the annual cycle. Stoyanov says a recruiter seat became supervision of the model, including owning the error, and that she re-scopes quarterly. Troyer describes one SEO specialist whose tasks moved to auditing generated content inside a quarter, with the title and band unchanged, and says a quarterly check of the three tasks that matter has caught two further drifts. Shah says Testlify, about 80 people, has not meaningfully reviewed job architecture since 2022, and that by his own estimate a third of roles have drifted. He says the brake is the annual budget calendar: mid-year evidence gets parked until the next cycle. Bali-Courtemanche disagrees with the reflex. She says AI should trigger an organisation review, not a string of one-off job evaluations, and that if the accountabilities have not materially changed, a new tool does not by itself justify a new level or a new rate. Spurling, a former CFO, makes the same cut from the budget side. A computer made a typist faster and did not make the typist more expensive. One person on her team now does work that took three people four years ago and still has capacity. She calls that a return on tools and training, not a reason to raise that person's pay.
- Number: the only figures that are the speakers' own, on this page, are Shah's "about 80" employees and his estimate that a third of roles drifted, Troyer's "two other drifts", and Spurling's "three people four years ago". None of those is a payroll extract.
- Grade: E3 for named people on the record. E1 for any general rule drawn from their companies. The vendor statistics they are wrapped in were not opened.
- Flag: Deel sells global employment services. Ruffini, Stoyanov, Spurling and Shah are founders of HR or software firms describing their own practice.
