## S1. Where a CFO survey says AI was least useful: scenario planning, model building, and variance

- Date: the page schema prints `datePublished` 7 October 2026. One WebPage node in that schema carries the id and URL of the Q2 report while the headline and the address are Q3.
- Source: https://www.pigment.com/reports/cfo-index/q3-2026
- Who: Pigment, a planning-software vendor. The page describes 2,000 CFOs and finance executives. The schema names the United States, the United Kingdom, France, and Germany. No Netherlands breakout. No response rate and no fieldwork date in the text that was returned.
- What: For every task asked, more people said AI was more useful than expected than said it was less useful. The chart of more-versus-less, and the net, is: budgeting 49.9% more and 7.8% less (net +42), reports and summaries 48.1% and 10.2% (+38), forecasting 44.2% and 10.5% (+34), data classification 43.9% and 11.9% (+32), internal reporting 42.1% and 11.5% (+31), variance analysis 41.0% and 12.6% (+28), model building 38.7% and 11.4% (+27), scenario planning 39.9% and 14.3% (+26). The prose says the least successful tasks are the ones that demand human judgement, and names scenario planning and model building.
- Number: the nets above. Variance +28, model building +27, scenario planning +26, forecasting +34. No Dutch slice.
- Grade: E3 as a published vendor survey. Self-reported.
- Flag: Pigment sells planning software and links its own product under the findings. Do not treat a positive net as a forecast error that got smaller. The finance-director note already uses the spend, staffing, governance, and reporting-hour charts on this URL. Those are not restated here.

## S6. A planning vendor's argument: the failure is a faster report of a plan nobody trusts

- Date: 25 September 2026. The page says last updated the same day.
- Source: https://www.solverglobal.com/blog/most-fpanda-teams-will-use-ai-to-do-the-wrong-thing-faster
- Who: Nils R., on Solver's blog. Solver sells planning software. This is an argument, not a survey.
- What: He predicts that most mid-market FP&A teams will spend the next two years making a weak planning process faster: rollover budgets, padding, silo plans, commentary written after the number, and a plan nobody opens until it breaks. "A 3% top-up on every line" is his description of the rollover habit, not a measured rate. Four agents are walked through as examples. The lines "38% above the internal benchmark," "a 15% increase in qualified pipeline," and "tracking 4% below plan" are dialogue inside those examples. He puts the window at about eighteen months, "through the end of 2027," and calls that his prediction. He also writes that agents do not fix missing drivers, unclean data, or a planning culture, and can make a broken process worse if that work is skipped.
- Number: none that the page measured. The 3%, 38%, 15%, and 4% figures are the author's examples. The eighteen months run to the end of 2027 on his clock.
- Grade: E3 as a published argument on a vendor's site. The figures inside the stories are not evidence.
- Flag: Solver wants the work he says teams are skipping. Do not cite 38%, 15%, or 4% as survey results. The failure mode is the part that is usable: a faster variance pack of a plan the business does not trust.
