## S1. The median vendor finishes 48% of engaged conversations; the best cluster near 70%

- Date: the opened page body did not show a publication date. A search listing said 6 October 2026. That date is not on the page.
- Source: https://www.saastr.com/how-much-of-your-customer-support-can-ai-really-resolve-the-best-get-about-70-the-median-is-48-heres-the-real-data-from-13-vendors/
- Who: SaaStr, byline blank. The benchmark is Gorgias's, on 212 live mid-market ecommerce stores. SaaStr Fund led Gorgias's seed. Gorgias ranks fifth. The underlying benchmark page was not opened. A marketing or sales manager buying a support agent is the reader. The clerk reading is in the 43-4 file.
- What: Automation rate is the share of engaged conversations resolved with no human, last four weeks. "Resolved" means zero human touch. Pushing the customer to email, a form, or a phone call counts against the agent. Gorgias's own customer reporting, as this page describes it, can count a customer who goes silent for 72 hours as resolved. This benchmark counts that as a failure. Gorgias AI Agent is priced per resolved conversation.
- Number: top five average 70%. Median 48%. Field 46% weighted by volume. Gorgias 64% (388 conversations). Price stated: $0.90 per resolved conversation. Decagon 73% on 34 conversations, which is a thin cell. Full table and four-week changes are in S2 and S3.
- Grade: E3 published benchmark, with a conflict of interest.
- Flag: the outlet's fund is an investor in the company that runs the board. Do not brief "AI resolves 70% of support" from this page. The median is 48%, and S3 says the rates fell.

## S2. The highest automation rate is not the highest quality score

- Date: same undated page as S1.
- Source: https://www.saastr.com/how-much-of-your-customer-support-can-ai-really-resolve-the-best-get-about-70-the-median-is-48-heres-the-real-data-from-13-vendors/
- Who: the same Gorgias benchmark, via SaaStr.
- What: A manager comparing vendors on one percentage will mis-rank them. The page prints a quality score beside the automation rate. The top automation rate has a middling quality score. A lower automation rate can have a higher quality score. Topic quality is higher on returns policy and shipping than on a shopper who is unsure what they want. Only about a third of post-sale conversations reach a finish. Judges rarely caught invented facts. They caught agents that would not engage. Nearly a third of chat widgets produced no real conversation.
- Number: Rep AI 75% automated, quality 55. Ada 68% and 39. Sierra 48% and 72. Klaviyo 21% and 65. Yuma 71% and 72. Topic quality: returns policy 77.4, shipping 74.4, shopper unsure 49.3. About a third of post-sale conversations reach a finish. Nearly a third of widgets produced no real conversation.
- Grade: E3, same conflict as S1.
- Flag: quality is this benchmark's score, not a CSAT from the stores. Do not export it as a brand metric.

## S3. Nine of ten vendors lost automation within four weeks

- Date: same undated page as S1.
- Source: https://www.saastr.com/how-much-of-your-customer-support-can-ai-really-resolve-the-best-get-about-70-the-median-is-48-heres-the-real-data-from-13-vendors/

- What: A launch-week rate is a bad sales forecast on this page. Nine of ten vendors with a history posted a lower automation rate four weeks later. Quality fell for nine of ten as well. One vendor moved the other way on automation. The page's failure list for a buyer: authentication walls, repeated demands for an order number, clarification loops, and handing everything to a person.

- Grade: E3, same conflict as S1.
- Flag: four weeks, ecommerce stores, one benchmarker's definition. Not a year of a brand's own customers.

## S4. Customers give the agent under ten minutes, and leaders guessed the wrong channel

- Date: 14 October 2026. Fieldwork April 2026.
- Source: https://www.cmswire.com/contact-center/contact-center-leaders-you-have-10-minutes/
- Who: Dom Nicastro, CMSWire, on a Five9-commissioned Hanover Research survey of 3,000 consumers in the US, UK, and Germany. The BusinessWire release was not opened. Five9 sells contact-centre software. The task table was truncated and is not cited row by row.
- What: For a marketing or sales manager who wants to move service into a chat widget: most respondents give an AI agent less than ten minutes, AI is the lowest-satisfaction channel on the page, and the most common reason for using it was that it was the only option. Leaders predicted Gen Z would prefer chatbots. Gen Z ranked chatbots near the bottom. Phone is the most preferred channel in every generation. About four in ten are less likely to use a company at all if it uses AI for customer service.
- Number: 92% under 10 minutes. AI satisfaction 46% positive, phone 68%, email and in-person about 64%. 54% say interactions shifted toward more AI over two years; 27% the reverse. 20% use AI most of the time or always; 38% only when they have to; 48% try to avoid it. Gen Z phone preference 23%, versus about two-thirds of the Silent Generation. About 4 in 10 less likely to use the company.
- Grade: E3 trade report of a commissioned survey.
- Flag: April opinions, October article. Five9's CEO is quoted on strategy. That quote is not a customer result. Password resets and order status are the only tasks where the fetched text says more than a third accept AI alone.

## S5. Trust moves when a person is visibly available, and people want to be told it is AI

- Date: 14 October 2026. Fieldwork April 2026.
- Source: https://www.cmswire.com/contact-center/contact-center-leaders-you-have-10-minutes/
- Who: the same Five9/Hanover survey, via CMSWire.
- What: The page's marketing-relevant mechanism is not a better model. It is a visible path to a human, and a disclosure that the respondent is talking to AI. Trust is higher with the path. Disclosure matters more to older respondents. A large minority do not trust AI with the security of what they type. Repeat-yourself handoffs are common. Misunderstanding is the main reason people leave, and it is higher among older respondents.
- Number: trust is 55% when a path to a person is visible and 26% when it is not. With the path, then without it: millennials 58% to 33%, baby boomers 56% to 16%, Silent Generation 57% to 5%. 71% say disclosure is very or extremely important (62% Gen Z, 91% Silent). 66% prefer a real person. 29% do not trust that information given to AI stays secure. 35% often or always repeat themselves after handoff. 46% leave because the bot repeatedly misunderstood (38% Gen Z, 59% boomers, 62% Silent).
- Grade: E3 commissioned survey, via trade press.
- Flag: the age splits are the page's. Do not smooth them into one "customers hate bots" line. The path-to-a-human result and the misunderstanding result point at different fixes.
