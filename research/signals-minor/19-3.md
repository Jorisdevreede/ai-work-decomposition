## S1. A model workflow flagged discrepancies in most replication packages it saw

- Date: September 2026 (issue date). DOI 10.3386/w35782.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35782
- Who: Matthew Schwartz, Isaiah Andrews, Jesse M. Shapiro, NBER working paper 35782. Abstract opened. PDF not opened. The computation-speed reading is in the 15-2 file. This item is the economist's audit of the literature.
- What: An open-source LLM workflow takes a replication package and reproduces, improves, and extends the article. Run across packages from five economics journals, it flags discrepancies in the article or the appendix in most of them. A flag is not, on the abstract, a confirmed error with a false-positive rate.
- Number: 4,452 published replication packages. Discrepancies flagged in 3,460 articles or their appendices.
- Grade: E3 abstract.
- Flag: 3,460 is a flag count, not a count of retracted papers. GitHub https://github.com/MetaEconomics was not opened.

## S2. The workflow wrote extensions the original papers do not contain

- Date: September 2026.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35782
- Who: Schwartz, Andrews, and Shapiro.
- What: In a subset of articles the workflow develops an extension aligned with the original goals that does not appear in the original. That is a different claim from finding a discrepancy. An economist using this as a research assistant would be accepting new analysis the authors did not publish, which the abstract says was checked for alignment with the original goals. The abstract does not describe a human referee step for each extension.
- Number: 923 articles with such an extension.
- Grade: E3 abstract.
- Flag: "aligned with the original goals" is the paper's standard. This note did not re-read an extension.

## S3. Some calculations got more than ten times faster

- Date: September 2026.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35782
- Who: Schwartz, Andrews, and Shapiro.
- What: In a smaller subset, the workflow reduces a calculation's computation time by more than ten times at similar or greater accuracy. For an applied economist the operational claim is runtime, not a new identification strategy. The abstract does not say the faster code was accepted by the journals.
- Number: 496 articles with a calculation more than 10× faster at similar or greater accuracy.
- Grade: E3 abstract.
- Flag: a threshold count, not an average speedup. Do not add 496 to 923 to 3,460 as if they were disjoint without the paper saying so. The abstract does not say they are disjoint.

## S5. Chat logs over-classify what social scientists, and everyone else, did

- Date: August 2026. DOI 10.3386/w35677.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35677
- Who: Alexander Bick, Adam Blandin, David J. Deming, Tyler R. Schumacher, NBER working paper 35677. Abstract only. This is economists measuring other occupations, which is itself social-science work, not a claim that economists automated their own papers. Paper 35782 is that claim.
- What: Exposure scores from occupational descriptions explain some of the variation in generative-AI adoption and far from all of it. Measures built from chat logs differ conceptually and tend to over-classify chats into generic activities. Adoption is widespread across occupations and tasks and shallow inside them. Workers doing similar work differ a lot.
- Number: within most occupations and tasks, fewer than half of workers adopt. No economist-specific share. A "45% of workers" line from a search snippet of the NBER homepage is not on this abstract and is not used.
- Grade: E3 abstract.
- Flag: PDF not opened. Acknowledgements name funders including the Walmart Foundation. Views are not necessarily those of the St. Louis Fed.
