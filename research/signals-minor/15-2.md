## S1. A 240% commit effect is 30% on releases

- Date: issued May 2026, revised September 2026.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35275
- Who: Mert Demirer, Leon Musolff, Liyuan Yang, NBER working paper 35275. Abstract opened. PDF not opened. Demirer and Musolff are paid research consultants for Microsoft and previously held Microsoft postdocs. The data-science reading is the substitution from commits to shipped work. The software-engineer reading is in the 15-1 file.
- What: Autocomplete, interactive coding agents, and autonomous coding agents have large cumulative effects on commits among more than 500,000 GitHub developers. The largest of those effects shrinks when the outcome is the number of projects, and shrinks again when the outcome is releases. The stated elasticity of substitution is well below 1. Four software marketplaces show more new apps and no increase in total usage.
- Number: commit effects +30%, +180%, +240%. The +240% falls to 80% for projects and 30% for releases. Elasticity of substitution 0.23.
- Grade: E3 working-paper abstract.
- Flag: do not cite sync-agent line counts from search snippets. They are not on the abstract. Do not blend with the Jellyfish percentages in the 15-1 file.

## S2. Replication code: faster calculations in 496 articles

- Date: September 2026 (issue date).
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35782
- Who: Matthew Schwartz, Isaiah Andrews, Jesse M. Shapiro, NBER working paper 35782. Abstract opened. PDF not opened. The economist reading of the same abstract is in the 19-3 file. This item is the computation result.
- What: An open-source LLM workflow reproduces, improves, and extends an economics article from its replication package. Across published replication packages for five economics journals, one result is a calculation whose runtime falls by more than ten times at similar or greater accuracy. The page says LLMs were used in the analysis and the writing. Schwartz worked as a contractor for Anthropic. Results are not endorsed by Anthropic.
- Number: 4,452 replication packages. In 496 articles, computation time reduced by more than 10× at similar or greater accuracy.
- Grade: E3 working-paper abstract.
- Flag: "more than 10×" is the paper's threshold count, not a median speedup. The GitHub repo named on the page was not opened. Economics articles are not an actuarial model inventory.

## S3. The same workflow flags discrepancies and writes extensions

- Date: September 2026.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35782
- Who: Schwartz, Andrews, and Shapiro. Abstract only.
- What: For a statistician or a data scientist who checks other people's code, the abstract also counts packages where a discrepancy was flagged in the article or its appendix, and articles where the workflow produced an extension aligned with the original goals that does not appear in the original. Those are different outcomes from the runtime cut in S2.
- Number: discrepancies flagged in 3,460 of 4,452 articles or their appendices. Extensions in 923 articles.
- Grade: E3 abstract.
- Flag: a flag is not a proven error. The abstract does not give a false-positive rate. Shapiro's other paid roles are on the page (Microsoft Research New England, FutureOfCapitalism, the New York Times) and are disclosures, not results.

## S6. Adoption inside an occupation is still a minority in most tasks

- Date: August 2026.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35677
- Who: Alexander Bick, Adam Blandin, David J. Deming, Tyler R. Schumacher, NBER working paper 35677. Abstract only.
- What: Exposure scores, the kind built from task text, explain some adoption and far from all of it. Chat logs tend to over-classify work into generic activities. Across occupations, use is widespread and shallow: within most occupations and tasks, fewer than half of workers adopt. That is a brake on any claim that "data scientists have automated the workbook."
- Number: fewer than half of workers, within most occupations and tasks. No actuary or statistician share is on the abstract. The 45% figure in a search snippet is not on this page and is not used.
- Grade: E3 abstract.
- Flag: not a census of this minor group. PDF not opened.
