# Signals: 43-4 Information and record clerks

Window: 17 July 2026 to 15 October 2026. Written 15 October 2026 by XSCOUT.
Each link below was opened in this session. Grades: E2 a named product does this today; E3 a documented case or survey you can read. Nothing here is E4. Vendor pieces are marked.

Covers customer service representatives, correspondence clerks, credit authorizers, eligibility interviewers, and file clerks (145 tasks). What shows up: vendors publish automation rates that do not mean the answer was right, customers give an agent a few minutes, and a visible path to a person changes trust. No opened page is this organisation's own ticket log.

## S1. On live stores, the median automation rate is 48%, and it slipped in four weeks

- Date: the opened page body did not show a publication date. A search listing said 6 October 2026. That date is not printed on the page.
- Source: https://www.saastr.com/how-much-of-your-customer-support-can-ai-really-resolve-the-best-get-about-70-the-median-is-48-heres-the-real-data-from-13-vendors/
- Who: SaaStr, byline blank on the fetched page. The benchmark is Gorgias's, run on 212 live mid-market ecommerce stores. SaaStr Fund led Gorgias's seed. The underlying Gorgias benchmark page was not opened.
- What: Automation rate means the share of engaged conversations resolved with no human, over the last four weeks. "Resolved" here means zero human touch. "Email us," a contact form, or "call us" count against the agent. If more than half of replies push the customer out, the conversation is scored unresolved. Gorgias's own customer reporting, described on this page, counts a resolution after the AI answers and 72 hours pass with no human, so a customer who gave up can count as resolved there and as a failure in this benchmark. Nine of ten vendors with a history lost automation over four weeks. Quality scores do not follow the automation ranking. Judges rarely caught made-up facts. They caught agents that would not engage. Nearly a third of "AI chat" widgets produced no real conversation. Only about a third of post-sale conversations reach a finish. Common failures: auth walls, repeated demands for an order number, clarification loops, handing everything to a person.
- Number: top five average 70%, median 48%, field 46% weighted by volume. Rates (conversation counts): Rep AI 75% (120), Decagon 73% (34), Yuma 71% (118), Ada 68% (420), Gorgias 64% (388), DigitalGenius 50% (561), Sierra 48% (408), Siena 46% (300), Kodif 44% (188), Intercom 42% (307), Zendesk 37% (467), Envive 22% (499), Klaviyo 21% (309). Four-week automation change: Siena −23, Kodif −13, Klaviyo −11, Sierra −11, DigitalGenius −10, Ada −9, Gorgias −6, Intercom −6, Zendesk −4, Envive +8. Quality examples: Yuma 72, Decagon 70, Gorgias 66, Sierra 72, Intercom 66, Klaviyo 65, Rep AI 55, Ada 39. Quality also fell for nine of ten (Ada −17, Zendesk −15, Sierra −14, Gorgias −12; Siena +2). Topic quality: returns policy 77.4, shipping 74.4, modify/cancel 63.5, order tracking 60.1, damaged item 59.7, shopper unsure 49.3. Gorgias AI Agent price on the page: $0.90 per resolved conversation. Mean reply time 5.4s (Envive) to 16.5s (Yuma).
- Grade: E3 as a published benchmark, with a conflict of interest.
- Flag: Gorgias ranks fifth on a board it runs, and the outlet's fund is an investor. Decagon's 34 conversations are a thin sample. Do not quote 70% without the median and the four-week drop.

## S2. Quality and automation are not the same ranking

- Who: same Gorgias benchmark, via SaaStr.
- What: The highest automation rate on the board has a middling quality score. A lower automation rate can sit next to a higher quality score. A clerk team buying on "percent resolved" alone would pick a different vendor from a team buying on answer quality.

## S3. Most people give an AI agent under ten minutes

- Date: 14 October 2026. Fieldwork was April 2026, before this window. The article is inside it.
- Source: https://www.cmswire.com/contact-center/contact-center-leaders-you-have-10-minutes/
- Who: Dom Nicastro, CMSWire, reporting a Five9-commissioned Hanover Research survey of 3,000 consumers in the US, UK, and Germany. The BusinessWire primary was not opened. The page was truncated at the task table; task-level rows past the sentences below were not used.
- What: Consumers describe a short fuse, repeat-yourself handoffs, and low satisfaction with AI compared with phone, email, and in-person. Trust is higher when a path to a human is visible. Disclosure that the other party is AI matters to most respondents, more so for older ones. The most common reason for using AI was that it was the only option. Leaders' guess about Gen Z's favourite channel did not match what Gen Z ranked.
- Number: 92% give an AI agent fewer than 10 minutes before escalating. 46% leave because the bot repeatedly misunderstood (38% Gen Z to 59% boomers, 62% Silent Generation). 35% often or always repeat themselves after handoff. AI/chatbot satisfaction 46% positive, lowest channel (phone 68%, email and in-person about 64%). 54% agree AI customer service is frustrating. Trust 26% to 55% when a path to a human is visible. 71% say disclosure is very or extremely important (62% Gen Z, 91% Silent). 66% prefer a real person. 29% do not trust that information given to AI stays secure. 20% use AI most of the time or always; 38% only when they have to; 9% never; 48% try to avoid AI. 54% say interactions shifted toward more AI over two years; 27% the reverse. Phone is the most preferred channel in every generation. Effective self-service ranked last at 12% among "what makes good service." About 4 in 10 are less likely to use a company at all if it uses AI for customer service. Password resets and order status are the only tasks where more than a third accept AI alone.
- Grade: E3 as trade journalism of a vendor-commissioned survey. The primary release was not opened.
- Flag: Five9 sells contact-centre software. April opinions are not October behaviour. Do not cite rows from the task table that were not in the fetched body.

## S4. "Automated resolution" can mean the customer never reached a person

- Date: 6 October 2026. Reviewed by Dominik Blattner.
- Source: https://www.kaizo.com/blog/how-to-qa-zendesk-ai-agents/
- Who: Kaizo, which sells quality assurance on support conversations. A Zendesk help-centre page and a Reddit thread cited on this page were not opened.
- What: Kaizo's definition, aimed at clerks and QA leads: an automated resolution in Zendesk means the customer did not reach a human, not that the answer was correct. Zendesk bills only for requests the AI resolved without escalation. Three failure modes are named. The transcript view, as Kaizo reports the help centre, shows the first 100 messages and only conversations inactive for more than 72 hours; Kaizo says that view is legacy and ends 10 December 2026.
- Number: billing rule as stated: no charge unless resolved with no human. Transcript limit: first 100 messages, and only after 72 hours idle. Legacy view end date: 10 December 2026. No accuracy rate is given for Zendesk's resolutions.
- Grade: E2. A vendor how-to. The billing sentence is Kaizo's reading of Zendesk.
- Flag: marketing. Kaizo's own UiPath and SteelSeries results on the same page are about QA time, not about AI-agent conversations, and are not used as resolution rates. The Reddit line about high-value customers is secondhand and is not quoted as fact.

## S5. Three ways a no-human ticket is still a bad ticket

- Date: 6 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.kaizo.com/blog/how-to-qa-zendesk-ai-agents/
- Who: Kaizo.
- What: The three failure modes on the page are: the customer gave up and silence counts as resolved; the answer was nearly right (right policy, skipped condition, or an old price); the bot promised something the company does not offer, such as a refund outside the window. A clerk sampling only "resolved" AI tickets will count all three as success unless someone reads the transcript.
- Number: none for how often each mode occurs.
- Grade: E2. Named failure modes in a vendor article, not a measured split.
- Flag: useful as a checklist. Not a rate.

## S6. A vendor's "up to" resolution gain is an execution count, not a finish

- Date: last modified 12 October 2026, as printed in Dutch on the page. Not necessarily the first publication date.
- Source: https://www.zendesk.nl/blog/ai/agentic-ai/customer-service-workflow-automation/
- Who: Courtney Burry, Zendesk. The Anthropic story cited for the execution count was not opened. The page stopped at "Test and monitor" in the fetch.
- What: For a customer-service clerk, the page's own note matters more than the headline: an execution is an agent run, not necessarily a completed resolution. Reported customer gains are "up to." An example workflow, attributed to Anthropic's description, identifies related incident tickets, opens a Jira issue, notifies engineers in Slack, updates customers, and closes tickets. An unnamed organisation's pilot merged duplicates.
- Number: "up to" 10% higher automated resolution. "Up to" 80% lower human handle times. 1 million executions in seven weeks, secondhand. Pilot: 99% duplicate-merge accuracy at an organisation with more than 14 million service requests a year. Five-person build team and four months are a product-engineering fact, filed under 15-1, not a clerk result.
- Grade: E2. Vendor marketing with a secondhand count.
- Flag: "up to" and an unnamed customer. Do not treat 1 million executions as 1 million resolved conversations. The page says they are not the same thing.

## S7. Staff starting with the agent still hit loops and dead ends

- Date: 8 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.microsoft.com/insidetrack/blog/building-trust-in-ai-powered-support-at-microsoft-our-employee-self-service-agent-story
- Who: Microsoft, on its internal Employee Self-Service agent. The reader here is the support clerk who receives what the agent does not finish, not the engineer who built it.
- What: Employees described a machine tone, troubleshooting loops, answers that ignored earlier attempts, and no clear handoff. One facilities/IT/HR example is Alex Calero on continuity over speed. After the redesign, Microsoft says fewer IT tickets are created and more people start with the agent. The clerk still exists for the rest.
- Number: 30% fewer IT support tickets created. 50% start with the agent, up from 27%. Population more than 300,000.
- Grade: E3 company self-report.
- Flag: no satisfaction figure and no statement of how many of the 50% still reach a person. The Windows Forum caveat (a ticket not created can be a person who gave up) is in the 15-1 file and applies here too.

## S8. Within most occupations, fewer than half of workers have adopted

- Date: August 2026 (issue date).
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35677
- Who: Alexander Bick, Adam Blandin, David J. Deming, Tyler R. Schumacher, NBER working paper 35677, "What Work Does Generative AI Do?" Abstract only. The PDF was not opened.
- What: A nationally representative survey links generative-AI adoption to occupations and tasks. Exposure scores explain some of the variation and far from all of it. Chat-log measures tend to over-classify chats into generic activities. Adoption is widespread across occupations and tasks, and shallow inside them. Customer-service exposure rankings built from job descriptions will overstate what clerks actually do with a tool.
- Number: within most occupations and tasks, fewer than half of workers adopt. No clerk-specific share is on the abstract. A homepage blurb about 45% of workers was not on this abstract and is not used.
- Grade: E3 as a working-paper abstract.
- Flag: not a contact-centre study. Use it as a brake on exposure scores, not as a resolution rate.
