Covers financial managers, controllers, treasurers, IT managers, facilities managers, administrative-services managers, and security managers (201 tasks). ## S1. IT management: fewer tickets after the agent was made easier to leave

- Date: 8 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.microsoft.com/insidetrack/blog/building-trust-in-ai-powered-support-at-microsoft-our-employee-self-service-agent-story
- Who: David Hirning, Microsoft Inside Track. The agent serves Microsoft's own employees and contingent staff. Designit interviews have no sample size.
- What: The management choice on the page is handoff and continuity, not a higher deflection target. Staff had hit loops and dead ends. After the redesign, Microsoft reports fewer IT tickets created and a higher share of people who start with the agent. The agent is described as covering IT, HR, or facilities.
- Number: more than 300,000 employees and contingent staff. 30% overall reduction in IT support tickets created. 50% start with the agent, up from 27%.
- Grade: E3 company self-report.
- Flag: Microsoft writing about Microsoft. No satisfaction score and no window for the 30%.

## S2. A 40% ticket target is not the 30% result

- Date: 8 October 2026.
- Source: https://windowsforum.com/news/microsoft-ess-ai-help-desk-trust-design-linked-to-reported-30-ticket-reduction.447679/
- Who: Windows Forum, on the Inside Track post. The author says it is a company case study, not an independent evaluation.
- What: For an IT manager reading a vendor case, the page separates a result from a target and notes that fewer tickets can mean people stopped asking. It also notes a channel limit.
- Number: result 30% (Microsoft's). Long-term target 40% fewer human-led tickets. About 250,000 vetted knowledge-base articles. Mobile does not yet support handoff. More than 300,000 people in 103 countries.
- Grade: E3 commentary. Not a second measurement.
- Flag: do not put 40% in a steering paper as if it had happened.

## S3. Enterprise buyers still name Copilot as the primary coding tool

- Date: 8 October 2026. Survey 4 August to 26 September 2026.
- Source: https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/ai-coding-assistant-market-share/
- Who: Evan Bailyn, First Page Sage. 2,350 professional developers, weighted by the agency against public seat counts. The IT-manager reading is the company-size split, not the developer horse-race (that is in the 15-1 file).
- What: In firms of 5,000 people or more, Copilot is the primary tool more often than Claude Code. In startups the order is reversed. A manager buying for a large IT estate is not looking at the same mix as a manager buying for a small one. The weighting method is the agency's and the microdata were not released.
- Number: enterprise 5,000+: Copilot 35.6% primary, Claude Code 22.4%, Cursor 8.3%. Startups: Claude Code 36.8%, Copilot 9.7%, Cursor 21.4%. Overall primary: Claude Code 29.4%, Copilot 22.7%.
- Grade: E3 published survey, agency methodology.
- Flag: primary tool, not seats in use. Do not quote the Stack Overflow multi-tool percentages (15-1) as this buying split.

## S4. Intake-to-pay, as a finance manager would time it

- Date: 31 August 2026.
- Source: https://ramp.com/blog/procurement-case-studies
- Who: Ramp, on Advisor360° (Ryan Williams) and Foursquare (Michael Bohn). Vendor cases.
- What: Two cycle stories on one page. Advisor360° is an approval and intake-to-pay clock plus a stated return. Foursquare is spend brought under management, rogue contracts, and a smaller software stack, after retiring a 20-page purchase-order guide and five tools. The buyer-level detail is in the 13-1 file.
- Number: Advisor360°: approval 10 days to 5.5 days, intake-to-pay cut 50%, 4x ROI in under a year, $80,000 savings. Foursquare: spend under management 60% to nearly 100%, zero rogue contracts, purchase order and invoice 4x faster, software stack cut more than 30%.
- Grade: E3 vendor cases.

## S5. Contact-centre leaders mis-predicted the channel people want

- Date: 14 October 2026. Survey fieldwork April 2026.
- Source: https://www.cmswire.com/contact-center/contact-center-leaders-you-have-10-minutes/
- Who: Dom Nicastro, CMSWire, on a Five9-commissioned Hanover Research survey of 3,000 consumers in the US, UK, and Germany. The BusinessWire release was not opened. Five9's CEO is quoted on product strategy; that quote is not a result.
- What: The management miss on the page: leaders expected Gen Z's top channel to be chatbots. Gen Z ranked chatbots near the bottom and preferred phone, live chat, and email. Phone is the most preferred channel in every generation. AI satisfaction is the lowest channel on the page. A visible path to a human is what moves trust.
- Number: 92% give an AI agent fewer than 10 minutes. Trust 26% to 55% when a path to a human is visible. AI satisfaction 46% positive, phone 68%. 71% say disclosure that they are talking to AI is very or extremely important. About 4 in 10 are less likely to use a company at all if it uses AI for customer service. Gen Z phone preference 23%, versus about two-thirds of the Silent Generation.
- Grade: E3 trade report of a commissioned survey.
- Flag: Five9 sells the category. April 2026 opinions. The task table was truncated and is not cited row by row.

## S6. An IT director's intake desk was email, spreadsheets, and the corridor

- Date: no publication date on the opened body. A search listing said 5 October 2026. That date is not on the page.
- Source: https://www.docusign.com/blog/how-ai-and-automated-workflows-transform-procurement-lessons-from-the-ymca-of-greater-philadelphia
- Who: Docusign, on Mark Morrison, Senior Director of IT, YMCA of Greater Philadelphia. The Deloitte PDF named on the page was not opened.
- What: The manager's before-state is hundreds of people as intake desks, plus spreadsheets, email, and hallway conversations. The product scans mail and attachments, recommends a request type, and pre-fills a form. Morrison's review-time claim is separate from the page's two different figures for how many requests now use one intake.

External counsel used to add five or seven days. Callout 99% of requests through one digital intake. Lede says 90% in one sentence.
- Grade: E3 vendor case.
- Flag: marketing. Keep 90% and 99% apart. No date on the body.

## S7. Code volume is up in a large-firm dataset; resolved work is not

- Date: 9 October 2026. Underlying data through March 2026.
- Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/ai-coding-agents-generate-more-code-but-not-more-software/
- Who: Kyle Orland, Ars Technica, on Chen and Stratton (Harvard) with Jellyfish data. The PDF was not opened. This item is the engineering-manager reading: output versus issues closed, and the review load. The developer reading is in the 15-1 file.
- What: Lines, commits, and pull requests rise. Jira issue and epic resolution does not, in a statistically significant way. Review time rises, more pull requests come back with changes requested, and more workers spend time reviewing. Employment does not change significantly in this dataset. By March 2026 most firms in the set used some AI code review.
- Number: +30% lines, +20% commits, +23% pull requests. Review time +49%. Comments per pull request +35%. Workers doing reviews +14%. Changes-requested share nearly doubled. 80% of firms used some AI code review. Agents wrote 23.3% of review comments and 10.8% of pull requests. More than 700 firms, more than 700,000 employees, 300 million work events.
- Grade: E3 news report of a research dataset. PDF not opened.
- Flag: cutoff March 2026. Not a headcount plan. Do not blend with the NBER release effects filed under 15-1 and 15-2.
