# Signals: 15-1 Computer occupations

Window: 17 July 2026 to 15 October 2026. Written 15 October 2026 by XSCOUT.
Each link below was opened in this session. Grades: E2 a named product does this today; E3 a documented case, paper, or survey you can read. Nothing here is E4. Vendor and marketing pieces are marked.

Covers computer systems analysts, support specialists, security analysts, network roles, and research scientists in this minor group (388 tasks). What shows up: coding agents raise lines, commits, and pull requests, while issue resolution and releases move less. Tool-share figures disagree because they measure different things. One internal IT desk published a ticket cut after a trust redesign. No opened page is this organisation's own engineering or support queue.

## S1. Coding agents add lines and reviews; resolved issues do not move

- Date: 9 October 2026.
- Source: https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/ai-coding-agents-generate-more-code-but-not-more-software/
- Who: Kyle Orland, Ars Technica, reporting Harvard researchers Fiona Chen and James Stratton, using Jellyfish data. The PDF at https://fion.ac/jellyfish.pdf was not opened.
- What: Across 300 million work events, more than 700,000 employees, and more than 700 firms, from 2021 to March 2026, AI coding agents are associated with more lines, commits, and pull requests. Resolution of Jira issues and epics is not statistically significant. Review time from pull request to merge rises. The share of pull requests with changes requested nearly doubles. There is no significant employment change. By March 2026 most of these firms used some AI code review, and agents wrote a minority of review comments and of pull requests.
- Number: +30% lines, +20% commits, +23% pull requests. Review time +49%. Comments per pull request +35%. Share of workers doing reviews +14%. Agents wrote 23.3% of review comments and 10.8% of pull requests. 80% of firms used some AI code review by March 2026.
- Grade: E3 via a news report of a research dataset. The paper PDF was not opened.
- Flag: data cutoff is March 2026, before this window. Do not read "more code" as "more software shipped." Do not blend these percentages with the NBER commit effects in S3 or the adoption shares in S2 and S4.

## S2. Adoption shares: Stack Overflow, JetBrains, and a marketplace count

- Date: 13 October 2026.
- Source: https://stackness.dev/blog/the-state-of-developer-stacks-q3-2026
- Who: Stackness, compiling other organisations' figures. The Stack Overflow and JetBrains survey pages were not opened. Stackness's own profile (n=22) is not used here.
- What: Stack Overflow's 2026 survey (fieldwork 23 June to 5 August, published 6 October; 30,903 responses, 12,255 on the agent question) has Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, Codex, and Cursor as the leading coding agents, and people can name more than one. JetBrains (May to July 2026, N=15,509, published 19 August) shows Claude Code rising sharply at work and Copilot falling. Microsoft's 29 July 2026 earnings call gives a Copilot user count and a revenue change. VS Code Marketplace installs from 1 July to 30 September move in the same direction as the surveys.
- Number: Stack Overflow: Claude Code 65.5%, Copilot 58.7%, Codex 29.5%, Cursor 25.5%. JetBrains at work: Claude Code 39% (from 3%), Copilot 21% (from 29%), Codex 16%, Cursor 12%. Microsoft: Copilot 50 million users, revenue up more than 60% quarter on quarter (the earnings note does not define "users"). Marketplace: Claude Code installs 19,278,058 to 26,682,153 (+38.4%); Copilot Chat +3.2%. Codex stars +35.2% to 127,213.
- Grade: E3 as a compiled report. Underlying survey pages were not opened.
- Flag: these are multi-tool adoption or install counts, not "primary tool" share. Do not average them with S4. The Copilot user figure is Microsoft's.

## S3. Commit effects shrink once the count is projects or releases

- Date: issued May 2026, revised September 2026. The revision date is the one inside this window.
- Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w35275
- Who: Mert Demirer, Leon Musolff, Liyuan Yang, NBER working paper 35275, "Writing Code vs. Shipping Code." The abstract page was opened. The PDF was not. Demirer and Musolff previously held Microsoft postdocs and are paid research consultants for Microsoft.
- What: On more than 500,000 GitHub developers plus AI usage telemetry, autocomplete, interactive coding agents, and autonomous coding agents have large cumulative effects on commits. The autonomous-agent effect is much smaller for the number of projects and smaller again for actual releases. Across four marketplaces the paper finds a sharp increase in new apps and no increase in total usage.
- Number: cumulative commit effects +30%, +180%, and +240% for the three modes. The +240% falls to 80% for projects and 30% for releases. Elasticity of substitution 0.23.
- Grade: E3 as a working-paper abstract.
- Flag: a different dataset from S1. A search snippet's sync-agent figures (+958% lines, +86% pull requests, +20% releases) are not on the abstract and are not used. Disclosure of the Microsoft link is on the page.

## S4. Primary-tool share is a stricter cut than adoption

- Date: 8 October 2026 (schema datePublished). Survey 4 August to 26 September 2026.
- Source: https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/ai-coding-assistant-market-share/
- Who: Evan Bailyn, First Page Sage, an SEO agency. Survey of 2,350 professional developers in North America and Europe, then weighted against public seat counts into their own "normalized model." Microdata were not released.
- What: Asked as the primary tool, not as any tool used, the top four still lead, and a terminal or CLI agent is a larger share of the interface mix than in January. Startups and large enterprises do not pick the same primary tool. The page also gives a model-family split for October.
- Number: primary tool: Claude Code 29.4% (+10.9 points vs January), Copilot 22.7% (−7.8), Cursor 13.1% (−4.9), Codex 11.8% (+7.6). Top four 77.0% (from 71.2% in January). Enterprise 5,000+: Copilot 35.6%, Claude Code 22.4%, Cursor 8.3%. Startups: Claude Code 36.8%, Copilot 9.7%, Cursor 21.4%. Interface: terminal/CLI agent 21.3% to 38.6%; IDE extension 41.2% to 29.4%. Model family: Claude 47.3%, GPT 28.6%, Gemini 10.2%, open-weight 9.4%, Grok 2.1%.
- Grade: E3 as a published survey, with the agency's weighting unreleased.
- Flag: do not blend 29.4% primary share with the 65.5% Stack Overflow adoption figure in S2. Quarterly averages on the same page differ slightly from the October snapshot.

## S5. An internal IT desk cut tickets after a trust redesign

- Date: 8 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.microsoft.com/insidetrack/blog/building-trust-in-ai-powered-support-at-microsoft-our-employee-self-service-agent-story
- Who: David Hirning, Microsoft Inside Track, on the Employee Self-Service agent used by Microsoft's own staff. Designit ran interviews; no sample size is given. Named staff: Olivier Cherel, Kevin Verdeck, David Finney, Chaitrasri Rao, Monserrat Oliver Sanchez, Alex Calero.
- What: The agent covers IT, HR, or facilities questions for employees and contingent staff. Early failures were a machine tone, troubleshooting loops, answers disconnected from earlier attempts, and an unclear way to reach a person. The redesign stressed continuity over speed and a visible handoff. Microsoft then reports fewer IT tickets created and a higher share of people starting with the agent.
- Number: more than 300,000 employees and contingent staff. 30% overall reduction in IT support tickets created. 50% of employees start their support journey with the agent, up from 27%.
- Grade: E3 as a company self-report.
- Flag: no comparison window and no satisfaction score. A second write-up of the same story is in S6 and adds caveats Microsoft does not print here. This is Microsoft writing about Microsoft.

## S6. The same ticket cut can also mean people gave up

- Date: 8 October 2026.
- Source: https://windowsforum.com/news/microsoft-ess-ai-help-desk-trust-design-linked-to-reported-30-ticket-reduction.447679/
- Who: Windows Forum, commenting on the Inside Track post in S5. An earlier May 2026 Europe North context on the same page is outside this note's use, except for the limits it records.
- What: The author treats the Inside Track piece as a company case study, not an independent evaluation, and says a fall in tickets can come from people giving up. The page also records a long-term target that is higher than the result Microsoft states, a large vetted knowledge base, and a mobile limit.
- Number: the 30% and the 50% are Microsoft's, repeated here. The page's own extras: about 250,000 vetted knowledge-base articles; a long-term target of 40% fewer human-led tickets (a target, not the result); mobile does not yet support handoff. Reach stated as more than 300,000 people in 103 countries.
- Grade: E3 as commentary on a company post. Not a second measurement.
- Flag: do not add the 40% target to the 30% result. Support specialists should read the handoff gap, not only the ticket line.

## S8. Spreadsheet agents still miss totals, pivots, and error cells

- Date: the opened page did not show an article date. The runs are dated 11 to 26 September 2026. A search listing said 6 October 2026; that date is not on the page.
- Source: https://gptforwork.com/blog/best-ai-model-for-excel-autosheetbench
- Who: GPT for Work, benchmarking its own Excel agent. Relevant here for analysts and developer-support staff who are asked to replace a workbook with an agent.
- What: 187 atomic spreadsheet tasks, one attempt each. The bench explicitly leaves out end-to-end financial models and dashboards. High scores still come with concrete misses: a waterfall that treated ending cash as a running figure, a formula block in place of a pivot, a cross-sheet trace with a mistyped reference, a missed #DIV/0! because the model used a partial preview, and region values dropped when sorting merged cells.
- Number: 187 tasks. Opus 5.5 Medium 94.7% (missed 10). GPT-6 Astra Low and Fable 5.1 Medium 92.0% (missed 15 each). Opus 4.8 High and GPT-6 Luna High 87.2% (missed 24). GPT-5.4 Low 81.3% (missed 35). Waterfall error: most models ended at €1.16M instead of €580K. Opus 5.5 native vs bulk 96% / 89%. 28 bulk operations were on 1,000-row sets; 159 tasks were native spreadsheet operations.
- Grade: E2. A vendor benchmark, not a customer deployment.
- Flag: the vendor sells the agent under test. Atomic tasks overstate what a month-end workbook needs. Credit and duration indexes are on the page and are not repeated as savings.
