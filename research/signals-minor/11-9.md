## S1. A construction firm took purchase orders out of the chief executive's inbox

- Date: 31 August 2026.
- Source: https://ramp.com/blog/procurement-case-studies
- Who: Ramp, on SAM Construction Group. James Hardy, CFO. Growth from 20 to more than 215 employees, 11 entities. The purchasing-clerk detail is in the 13-1 file. This item is the construction manager's before-and-after: email and Excel as the system of record.
- What: Purchase orders had arrived in a chief executive's inbox. Card spend sat in Excel and was chased for up to two months. Ramp says the workflow is now in the product, approvals are faster, and money was recovered.
- Number: approval 12.5 days faster. More than $800,000 recovered annually. More than 72% of accounts-payable spend through Ramp. 4,200+ purchase orders. Chase time up to 2 months.
- Grade: E3 vendor case.
- Flag: marketing. "Recovered" is Ramp's figure. No project-cost audit is linked in the fetched text.

## S2. Primary senior leaders moved parent messages to AI; data analysis did not follow

- Date: 6 October 2026.
- Source: https://teachertapp.com/articles/disruption-by-year-group-planning-time-ai-predictions-vs-reality/
- Who: Teacher Tapp, an England teacher panel. The item for education administrators is the senior-leader series. Classroom-teacher series are in the 25-2 file. Charts are images; only prose numbers are used.
- What: The page compares what staff in 2023 said they expected to do with AI against what they report now. Parent communications rose for senior leaders. Uses that sound more managerial, such as analysing attainment or behaviour data, fell. Marking in secondary also fell for leaders as well as teachers.
- Number: parent communications, primary senior leaders, up 28 points, about 28% to 56%. Secondary senior leaders up 10 points. Behaviour data: secondary leaders down 26 points. Marking in secondary down 24 points for leaders, from about a third to under 10%.
- Grade: E3 independent panel.
- Flag: England. Self-report. "About 28% to 56%" is the page's wording for the primary-leader move. Do not treat a parent-letter gain as a data-analysis gain. The page shows the opposite on attainment and behaviour.

## S3. A lesson-planning tool saved time in a trial, and use fell during the trial

- Date: 6 October 2026. Fieldwork was the autumn 2025 term, ten weeks.
- Source: https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/ai-tool-reduced-primary-teachers-lesson-planning-time-by-a-quarter-but-views-on-the-tool-were-mixed
- Who: Education Endowment Foundation press release. Oak National Academy's Aila. Evaluation by NFER. The administrator reading is adoption and purchase, not the minute count (that is in the 25-2 file). Becky Francis is quoted that providing tools does not lead to sustained adoption. The evaluation PDF was not opened.
- What: A randomised trial in English primary schools found less weekly planning time in the Aila group, with a high security rating from EEF, and quality not rated lower on this press release. Use declined over the ten weeks. After the trial, fewer than one in five of the Aila group felt the tool had meaningfully influenced teaching and planning. Experienced teachers preferred adapting plans they already had. Aila builds lessons from scratch. The comparison group could use other AI and was told not to use Aila.
- Number: 464 teachers, 108 schools. 81% of the Aila group used it at least once. Fewer than one in five felt it meaningfully influenced teaching and planning. Frequency and amount of use declined over ten weeks. £2.5 million is named as an EEF research fund on the page, not as the cost of Aila.
- Grade: E3 press release of an independent evaluation. PDF not opened.
- Flag: a time saving that fades is the management fact. Do not quote the 24% time cut from the 25-2 file as if teachers kept the habit. A 42-resource review figure was not on this press release and is not used.

## S4. A farm adviser says to try AI on notes before a spray or fertility decision

- Date: byline 4 October 2026. The page chrome also says 14 October 2026.
- Source: https://www.agrinews-pubs.com/community-contributed/2026/10/04/extension-notebook-ai-in-agriculture/
- Who: Dennis Bowman, University of Illinois Extension specialist, digital agriculture. Written for farmers and ranchers. No measured deployment on the author's own clients is given.
- What: Examples of lower-risk uses: telling horseradish from other plants so equipment can target weeds where few herbicides are labelled; turning drone imagery of trees, buildings, and water into an application map and a flight route for a spray drone; drafting a scouting report or sorting field notes. A photo passed to a generative model can suggest nutrient deficiency, drought, or pest damage. Bowman says those are possibilities, not a diagnosis, and warns about hallucination. An Illinois nitrogen-management experiment, date not stated as inside this window, is the failure case: an algorithm cut nitrogen and the yield loss outweighed the fertiliser saving. His advice is to try the lower-risk work before a fertility or spray decision. Agentic AI is described as a concept, not as a product with a result.
- Number: yield loss of 33 bushels per acre in the nitrogen experiment, as Bowman states it. The original study was not opened. No acreage, no dollar figure, and no adoption rate.
- Grade: E3 as an extension column. The 33 bushels figure is restated, not re-measured here.
- Flag: the experiment's date is not given. Do not treat a horseradish classifier or a spray-drone route as a result from this autumn. The technician reading is in the 19-4 file.
