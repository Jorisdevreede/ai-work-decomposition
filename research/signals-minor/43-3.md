## S1. A day of typing quote PDFs into purchase orders

- Date: 31 August 2026.
- Source: https://ramp.com/blog/procurement-case-studies
- Who: Ramp, on Precision Neuroscience. Brian is named as financial controller. The clerk task on the page is the retype.
- What: The old path was a spreadsheet, then retyping a quote PDF into a formal purchase order. The new path uses OCR and routes the purchase order. Ramp says that had been an entire day of data entry, on a weekly batch of purchase orders, and that routing got faster. Four tools became one.
- Number: an entire day of data entry before. 20 to 30 purchase orders a week. 50%+ faster purchase-order routing. 1 to 2 days saved on month-end close, which is the close figure and is not a clerk headcount.
- Grade: E3 vendor case.
- Flag: marketing. "An entire day" is Ramp's description. No timesheet is shown.

## S6. Spreadsheet agents missed the kinds of errors a clerk is paid to catch

- Date: runs 11 to 26 September 2026. The opened article did not show a publication date.
- Source: https://gptforwork.com/blog/best-ai-model-for-excel-autosheetbench
- Who: GPT for Work, benchmarking its own Excel agent. 187 atomic tasks, one attempt. Not a bookkeeping deployment.
- What: Failures that match clerk work: a waterfall that did not tie because ending cash was not a total; a model that built formulas instead of a pivot; a missed #DIV/0! because the model used a partial preview; region values dropped when sorting merged cells. High overall scores sit next to those misses.
- Number: wrong cash figure €1.16M instead of €580K for most models. Opus 5.5 Medium 94.7% (missed 10 of 187). GPT-5.4 Low 81.3% (missed 35), and GPT-5.4 is the model named for the false pivot and the missed #DIV/0!. Luna and GPT-5.4 lost region values on the merged-cell sort.
- Grade: E2 vendor benchmark.
- Flag: the vendor sells the agent. Atomic tasks. A 94.7% score is not a month of purchase invoices posted correctly.
