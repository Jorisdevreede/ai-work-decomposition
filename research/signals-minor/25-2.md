## S1. Primary teachers using Aila spent about 49 minutes less a week on planning

- Date: 6 October 2026. Fieldwork: autumn 2025 term, 10 weeks, England, Key Stage 2 (ages 7 to 11).
- Source: https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/ai-tool-reduced-primary-teachers-lesson-planning-time-by-a-quarter-but-views-on-the-tool-were-mixed
- Who: Education Endowment Foundation press release. Tool: Oak National Academy's Aila. Evaluator: NFER. Quotes on the page from Becky Francis (EEF), Ben Styles (NFER), and John Roberts (Oak). The PDF was not opened: https://d2tic4wvo1iusb.cloudfront.net/production/documents/projects/aila-lesson-planning_teacher-choices_evaluation-report.pdf
- What: Teachers were randomised. The Aila group reported less weekly planning time than the comparison group. The comparison group could use other AI tools and was told not to use Aila. EEF gives the trial a high security rating. The press release says quality was not lower. Aila makes new lessons from scratch. Experienced teachers preferred adapting plans they already had. Newer teachers and teachers less confident in the subject gained more.
- Number: 464 teachers, 108 schools. Aila group: two and a half hours a week. Comparison: three hours and 19 minutes. About 49 minutes a week, 24% less. The Aila group spent 76% of the comparison group's time.
- Grade: E3 press release of an RCT. The PDF was not opened, so figures that are not in the release are not used.
- Flag: self-reported time. Autumn 2025 fieldwork, October 2026 release. A figure of 42 resources reviewed was in a search snippet of the PDF and is not on this release.

## S2. Use of the same tool fell during the ten weeks

- Date: 6 October 2026.
- Source: https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/ai-tool-reduced-primary-teachers-lesson-planning-time-by-a-quarter-but-views-on-the-tool-were-mixed
- Who: EEF, reporting the NFER evaluation of Aila.
- What: Most of the Aila group opened the tool at least once. Frequency and amount of use declined over the ten weeks. In a post-trial survey, fewer than one in five felt the tool had meaningfully influenced their teaching and planning. Francis's line on the page is that providing a tool does not lead to sustained adoption, and that this is not a silver bullet. The time cut in S1 and the fade here are the same trial.
- Number: 81% used Aila at least once. Fewer than one in five felt it meaningfully influenced teaching and planning. Decline over 10 weeks, with no week-by-week table on the release.
- Grade: E3, same release as S1.
- Flag: do not quote the 24% without this fade. The £2.5 million named on the page is an EEF research fund, not a price per school.

- Number: ChatGPT users spent 69% of comparison-group time, as this release states it. No teacher count for that trial is repeated on the sentences used here.
- Grade: E3 as a 2026 restatement. Not a re-read of the 2024 report.

## S4. Lesson planning with AI rose; it is still near the 2023 guess

- Date: 6 October 2026.
- Source: https://teachertapp.com/articles/disruption-by-year-group-planning-time-ai-predictions-vs-reality/
- Who: Teacher Tapp, England panel. "AI predictions vs reality" compares expected use in 2023 with reported use now. Charts are images. Prose numbers only.
- What: For primary classroom teachers, reported use of AI for lesson planning is higher than the old prediction. Secondary is only a little above the prediction. A separate, non-AI question on the same page says planning time is still a large share of the lesson: the most common answer for the last lesson taught is a quarter of the lesson length.
- Number: primary classroom teachers, lesson planning, up 16 points, 39% to 55%. Secondary above the prediction by 5 points. Most common planning answer: 25% of lesson time (15 minutes on a 60-minute lesson). 13% of Key Stage 2 teachers spent 100% or more of the lesson length planning it.
- Grade: E3 panel.
- Flag: the planning-time percentages are not an AI effect. They are the workload the AI items sit on. Self-report.

## S5. Marking, homework, and attainment analysis are below what teachers expected

- Date: 6 October 2026.
- Source: https://teachertapp.com/articles/disruption-by-year-group-planning-time-ai-predictions-vs-reality/
- Who: Teacher Tapp.
- What: Uses that replace judgement on pupil work moved the other way from lesson planning. AI analysis of attainment data is far below the 2023 expectation. Marking in secondary is far below it. Homework is down in primary and secondary. The page's phrase for behaviour-data use among secondary teachers is that almost nobody is doing it.
- Number: attainment data, primary teachers, down 27 points, 34% to 7%. Secondary teachers down 25 points on attainment. Behaviour data: secondary leaders down 26 points; secondary teachers "down to 24 points," which the page glosses as almost nobody. Marking in secondary down 24 points for both teachers and leaders, from about a third to under 10%. A later sentence: 7% of secondary teachers used AI for marking, against 31% who thought they would. Homework is down in both phases. No homework percentage is in the prose.
- Grade: E3 panel.
- Flag: "down 26 points" (leaders, behaviour) and "down to 24 points" (teachers, behaviour) are different wordings. Do not turn the second into a 24-point drop. The 7% and the "under 10%" both describe secondary marking and are both on the page. Do not read the chart images for numbers that are not in the prose.
